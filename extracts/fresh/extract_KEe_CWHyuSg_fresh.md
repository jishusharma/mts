## KEY FINDINGS

1. Capital markets are about which side has the upper hand: buyers, sellers, or balance.
2. The volume profile indicates market phases with high liquidity and market phases with no liquidity.
3. High-liquidity areas can be viewed as balanced market phases where traders exchanged.
4. Low-liquidity areas can be viewed as fast market phases where price zips through or reverses back to equilibrium.
5. The market tends to stop at the last price range of highest turnover before entering a low volume node.
6. In the S&P 500 example, turnover was declining; when turnover declines, supply and demand are no longer being played excessively, which suggests a pullback first.
7. Rising VPOCs with increasing liquidity indicate a stable, bullish structure.
8. Single print areas represent fast moves that are often corrected later.
9. A buying surplus with volume dropping indicates the market is no longer being priced fairly, suggesting a reaction or pullback is likely.
10. The Nasdaq example looked "like a surface-to-surface rocket" with no substance; very steep moves with little liquidity indicate a high probability of a turn.
11. Volume profile can locate the likely turn, but timing requires waiting for absolute exhaustion: a volume spike or high volume at one price then a drop below the balance.
12. Bitcoin is used as a leading indicator for risk appetite; inflationary concerns and whether money continues flowing into riskier assets will show where the market is headed.
