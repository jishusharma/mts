## KEY FINDINGS

1. Fair value gaps are price areas where the market has not made pullbacks during a fast, accelerated trend.
2. There are three trend structure types: fast trend, normal trend, and slow creeping trend.
3. Gaps get filled because no fair auction occurred and one side was dominant; large participants may still have open auctions at those levels.
4. When price returns to a fair value gap, the expected first reaction is often a range, not an immediate countertrade.
5. A fair value gap is not an entry trigger by itself. Trader must know the next target area to build a valid risk-reward ratio.
6. Probability is higher that price first returns to the next fair price area before revisiting an unfair price level.
7. In a fast trend with a pullback and new setup, a stop below the pullback can target new highs because the prior trend is not likely to act as resistance.
8. If the market has turned into balance/sideways and then moves down, the same fair value gap entry can have only a 1:1 risk-reward ratio.
9. An old single print area at 23,660 is a fair value gap, but the probability of a strong first reaction there is small because it was an open drive with a strong buying tail and no support.
10. If price falls below an area with no trading activity, it tends to drop to the next price area where real volume occurred.
11. Breakout setup to the short side can be taken because it may trade through the entire zone.
12. Stop-loss placement using footprint logic can improve the risk-reward ratio significantly.
