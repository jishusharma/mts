## KEY FINDINGS

1. A candlestick contains four core data points: open, close, maximum high, and absolute low.
2. The market open and closing price are information-rich because participants enter within roughly 40-45 minutes after open, and ETFs must close positions in the closing auction.
3. Long wicks indicate rejection, but a long upper wick plus continued selling in the next candle shows seller dominance.
4. An inside bar shows indecision; when it follows a three-in-a-line, a break back above the prior point increases the probability of trend reversal.
5. Three-in-a-line, three same-color candle bodies, shows impulsive selling and often leads to a brief pullback then continued dynamic downward movement.
6. A hammer combined with a predefined price range such as yesterday's closing price is treated as a trend reversal setup.
7. A bearish engulfing/outside bar after an upward move is a reversal formation; when combined with support/resistance, it is "technical proof" of a pre-localized price range.
8. Tom uses candlestick patterns to build "PB concepts"; after strong impulsive buying and a balance phase, a candle closing at its highs above consolidation suggests trend continuation.
