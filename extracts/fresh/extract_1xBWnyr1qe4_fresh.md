2. Time-and-sales flow is compared to water: continuous red prints show aggressive selling.
3. Large red prints with thousand-contract size plus bid prices dropping indicate an aggressive seller.
4. Support and resistance are only real after active aggressive trading; a drawn line alone is not support/resistance.
5. Fast moves into resistance with aggressive bid/ask and volume suggest breakout; slow moves with no transaction suggest reversal.
6. Scalping is described as the best way to learn market structure, though not the easiest way.
7. Backtesting is rejected because the market is dynamic and changes over time.
8. The biggest personal problem is ego: wanting to be right and fighting the market.
9. Retail stop-loss hunting is largely a misconception; institutions and algorithms hunt large liquidity, not small retail stops.
10. Algorithmic market making has replaced pit traders; algorithmic speed is the main change, not the market structure itself.
