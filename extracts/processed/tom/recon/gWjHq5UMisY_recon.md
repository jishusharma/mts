NO TRADE WALKTHROUGH
- Day trading takes "much smaller distances", so "the probability is many times higher that your goals will be reached", and you can trail stops because you are in the market the whole time.
- In swing trading you enter "at the end of the day or on the trading day when the market opens" and are "often not at your desk at all", so the market can miss its goal even after being far in profit.
- Day-trading prep stack: 30-minute chart for price structure, weekly Market Profile (previous week value/equilibrium), VPOC location ("the volume weighted point of control"), intraday profile for balance, then a 5-minute chart for balance top/bottom and reversal vs trend-continuation setups.
- Final confirmation is a footprint chart combined with the order book for turnover/liquidity and overall market speed.
- Swing example: SpaceX trades well below the IPO price after a strong sell-off and pullback; the profit target is the return to the IPO price, and "the risk-reward ratio is many times larger than is usually the case in day trading".
- Swing win rates are much lower (example "38%") but the R:R is much larger, versus "61 or 63%" in day trading.
- At the illustrated R:R of 2.2 the theoretical breakeven is "a win rate of 32%" without fees; a 50% win rate at 2.2 R:R gives "0.6R per trade".
- Two levers to improve: "increase these stretches here" (bigger winning stretches) or "work on your setup so that your stop loss becomes smaller".
- Time expenditure: end-of-day analysis with a "very clearly defined target area" suits part-time traders better than high-frequency day trading.
- Theoretical long illustration: entry via balance at 119, stop under the POC at 114, opportunity/target 130, risk $5 vs $11 reward, R:R "2.2".
