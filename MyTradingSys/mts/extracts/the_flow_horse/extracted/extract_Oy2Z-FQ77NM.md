> "It doesn't tell us about who bought or who sold, whether or not those positions were bought to close or bought to open... whether or not it's longs or shorts, whether they're a directional trader, a short-term speculator, a long-term speculator, whether or not they are delta neutral..."
> — Oy2Z-FQ77NM, ~01:45

**Context:** General principle about the limits of volume profile as an analytical tool. Applies to all market conditions.

## RULE 2: PROXIMITY PRINCIPLE — SHORT-TERM PROFILES > LONG-TERM FOR POSITIONING
> "The closer we are in proximity to where that turnover took place, the more likely that positioning is still relevant. If it took place 5, 15 minutes ago, a session ago even, it's probably still more relevant. Whereas, if something took place 5 months ago again, we're making a lot of assumptions..."
> — Oy2Z-FQ77NM, ~06:00

> "The only way we could say much about positioning... is day one, is day two, is day three, and then it begins to drift off as we move further back."
> — Oy2Z-FQ77NM, ~16:45

**Context:** Time-decay of profile relevance. Recent profiles (daily, weekly) more actionable than old ones (monthly, quarterly).

## RULE 5: HEAD FAKE SIGNATURE — LACK OF VOLUME/MOMENTUM ON BREAK
> "This is a head fake... You look for a lack of volume, a lack of participation, a lack of momentum. You might even look for very obvious levels of absorption. So, what buying comes in is immediately stuffed. The setup for this is relatively simple, especially if it's on the first or second go. You have a retest of a level, level is swept, you see that volume came in and above the level... The market trades back below that, trades back within previous value."
> — Oy2Z-FQ77NM, ~08:00

> "If there's any sign of weakness, it's when price is just drifting lower. You want punchy, minimal selling, punchy again."
> — Oy2Z-FQ77NM, ~08:15

**Context:** False break identification: low volume + absorption + immediate rejection back into value = head fake. "Drifting" after break = weakness, not conviction.
