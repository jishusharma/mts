## 3. Which clear, which stay blocked, what floor admits them
- **Engine targets vs floor 1.25:** 529 of 930 episodes clear it (57%), 401 are blocked (nrr < 1.25). At 1.0: 565; at 1.5: 432; at 2.33: 202. Quantiles of engine nrr p10/p25/p50/p75/p90 = 0.04 / 0.38 / 1.41 / 2.08 / 3.81.
- **The blocked ones are blocked by a target-choice, not by the floor:** at the nearest node the median net_rr is 0.01 (only 12 of 904 reach 1.0); node 2 median 0.18 (33 reach 1.25); node 3 median 0.38 (71 reach 1.25). A floor that admits the nearest-node targets would have to be ~0 to 0.4,
  below break-even at any hit rate under 50%. Confirms the Cloud finding (appendix A): G1 is target selection, not floor level. 188 episodes have no node clearing 1.25 at all; the median first clearing node is the 5th-6th level away (a runner, not a "next node").
- **Cloud candidates:** A/B/C are 10-05 rows; this run does not re-derive them one by one, but the same pattern holds (nearest node < 1.0 in 892 of 904 episodes).
- **Do the engine-clearing candidates win?** No, on this window. Engine-target hit rate by engine nrr bucket: [1.25,1.5) 5%, [1.5,2.33) 3%, [2.33,inf) 9% (decided trades), mean net R -0.88 / -0.90 / -0.62; break-even hit for those buckets is 42% / 36% / 20%.
  Every bucket's observed hit rate is below its break-even. Nearest-node targets hit 50% but at ~0 R, mean net R -0.46 (cost dominates). **No R:R floor, 1.25 or 2.33, makes these candidates positive**; raising or lowering it only selects which losers trade.
  The [-inf,0) bucket (hit 0.90) is an artifact: target inside the round-trip cost, so it "hits" at negative R.
- **Reading:** with entries at zone touch and these stops, far targets are rarely reached within 12 h in this window (a ranging/mean-reverting day-pair; sample is two days, one regime). Treat as a diagnosis of where the R:R number comes from (distant node over tight stop), not as a measured edge or a floor recommendation.
  The sourced floor remains (1-p)/p with measured p (TOM-093); the p measured here (0.03-0.09 at the engine target) is the number to look at, and it is far below any sourced band.

## 4. Caveats
1. Same-bar stop/target ties go to the stop (conservative); 1m bars, no queue/slippage model; entry assumed at zone price, which may not fill (nofill 269-300 of 930).
2. 12 h horizon and 60 min fill window are replay conventions, unsourced (research-only), not tuned.
3. Episodes overlap (same level re-alerted across hours); counts are not independent. Hit rates carry no CI here.
4. Nodes beyond entry are many and clustered (median 5th-6th node to clear 1.25), so "next node" is ambiguous; results for node k are shown for k=1..3 only.
5. Stops from the snapshots are the engine's own; no stop-width study was run.
6. Snapshot timestamp (UTC) used for alignment; file names are local date.

## 4. Caveats

1. Three candidates, hand-transcribed from a doc, one of them (C) with an approximated stop; this is a worked check, not a sample. No hit-rate conclusion can come from it.
2. C was not blocked by R:R at 13:42 (reported nrr 2.68 >= 1.25, state WAIT_TRIGGER); the 13:31 miss was G12 (zone touch lasted less than one 1-min cycle) as much as G1.
3. The reported 13:42 nrr 2.68 is close to node-4 net 2.76 at a 190-pt stop; the engine's actual target/stop for that row are not on the ref, so this is consistent, not confirmed.
4. 13:48 ct_short @ daily_vah (nrr 1.71, MA:134) and the blocked 4h_vah short (0.69) are as reported; their stops/targets are not documented, so they were not recomputed.
5. Whether "next node beyond the nearest" is Tom's rule is itself thin: TOM-134 says plan "from one level to the next" and "from here to the next major volume"; it names no skip-the-nearest rule. Treat it as a hypothesis to test, with the nearest node as the control.
