## Rules

### R1. Patterns only work where the structure is stable (SOURCED)

Rule: Continuation and breakout patterns and trend lines work when the market has a stable structure because players accept higher prices step by step.

> "But this structure prevails because the players are accepting higher prices step by step."
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | BEGIN**

### R2. Where consolidation and acceleration happened (SOURCED)

Rule: What matters is where the market consolidated and where it accelerated, not where a trend line can be drawn.

> "Isn't it much more important to know where markets have consolidated and where they've accelerated strongly?"
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | BEGIN**

### R3. A trend line shows no supply and demand (SOURCED)

Rule: A trend line is only a movement pattern; it does not show where big players entered or defend price areas.

> "But it doesn't show you where supply and demand are where big players have actually entered the market and possibly have an interest in defending these price areas."
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | BEGIN**

### R4. Averaging against an overbought indicator (SOURCED)

Rule: Shorting every new high because an oscillator is overbought and averaging down is how accounts are wiped out when the cause is a real driver such as news.

> "And now comes the best part. Then some people even average down their positions and make them bigger because they want to be right."
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | MIDDLE**

### R5. Three setup families (SOURCED)

Rule: Separate counter-trend, trend-following and breakout setups; searching for the definitive breakout inside a range fails.

> "if you consistently keep looking for, say, the definitive breakout setup within a particular trading range, well, then good night."
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | MIDDLE**

### R6. Support and resistance need participant logic (SOURCED)

Rule: Support and resistance must be combined with who initiated the move and which groups keep the market still or trending.

> "It is essential to always combine the technical analysis concepts of support and resistance with the corresponding overarching global player logic."
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | MIDDLE**

### R7. Dominant participant first (SOURCED)

Rule: First identify who is dominating the market now; it usually starts much earlier than the visible explosion.

> "And that usually doesn't start where we think, "Oh, this is where things are exploding. Now the big players are in the market.""
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | MIDDLE**

### R8. Seller surplus read through speed (SOURCED)

Rule: Seller dominance can be observed through the speed of transactions, combined with the price range.

> "dominated by sellers for instance through the observable speed of transactions"
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | END**

### R9. High volume without progress is absorption (SOURCED)

Rule: Large volume with no further rise means absorption; the order book then shows who is done.

> "There were large volumes up here. Yes, but why didn't the market keep rising then? Because there was no more momentum. Absorption took place."
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | END**

### R10. Big buyer eaten then impulse (SOURCED)

Rule: When the big resting buyer is eaten and the impulse follows, that is the continuation signal (combination of context and dynamics).

> "And there we can see how the big buyer suddenly gets eaten up. The impulse comes and there's a continuation."
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | END**

### R11. Order of questions (SOURCED)

Rule: Order: who dominates, where was it dominated, which trader groups are active, then use dynamics for the entry.

> "Who is dominating the market? Where was the market dominated? Which trader groups are currently active?"
**Source GPS: 9VN9y2DVtGQ | END**
