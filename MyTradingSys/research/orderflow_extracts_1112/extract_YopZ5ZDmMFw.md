## Rules

### R1. Stable versus fast trend (SOURCED)

Rule: A stable trend repeatedly pauses in balance so participants can build positions; a trend that just crashes without balance is easily negated.

> "a stable trend is a trend that repeatedly has phases of balance. This means that market participants have enough time to take their positions, which gives the trend a certain stability."
**Source GPS: YopZ5ZDmMFw | BEGIN**

### R2. Look left for obstacles (SOURCED)

Rule: Check what technical obstacles sit to the left before trading a resistance or waiting.

> "I always look to the left and check what technical obstacles are there."
**Source GPS: YopZ5ZDmMFw | BEGIN**

### R3. Big picture first, in balance stays in balance (SOURCED)

Rule: Start with the weekly profile: if price sits in the middle of last week's balance, expect it to stay in balance and do not hunt impulses.

> "if I'm in the middle, then I don't need to look for any impulsive moves because the probability is high that if something is in balance, it will stay in balance for now."
**Source GPS: YopZ5ZDmMFw | BEGIN**

### R4. Higher time frames show where big money is (SOURCED)

Rule: Large players read larger time frames, not minute charts; weekly value area high and low say whether price is inside or outside balance.

> "the global players don't look at minute charts. Instead, they look at the larger time frames where they can get information."
**Source GPS: YopZ5ZDmMFw | MIDDLE**

### R5. Gap-close speculators wrong (SOURCED)

Rule: If price dips after a gap and then finds balance above, those who bet on a gap close were wrong and supply and demand moved to a new range (index-futures opening gap, not a BTC rule).

> "It tells me that the people who speculated down here on gap closes were initially wrong."
**Source GPS: YopZ5ZDmMFw | MIDDLE**

### R6. Time spent = acceptance (SOURCED)

Rule: Time spent at higher prices means participants accept them.

> "market participants are accepting these higher prices because they are spending a lot of time here."
**Source GPS: YopZ5ZDmMFw | MIDDLE**

### R7. Incomplete profile gets repaired (SOURCED)

Rule: Naked POCs, old single prints and old value areas are target areas; an incomplete old profile is likely repaired first.

### R8. Context before setup (SOURCED)

Rule: Ask if the setup fits the context of where big money is; do not search for the thousandth breakout or counter-trade in isolation.

> "Instead, I always have to check does this fit in terms of context with the big money?"

### R9. Dynamics at the level decide counter versus slide (SOURCED)

Rule: At a single-print zone, volume and speed decide whether to counter-trade or expect continued sliding.

> "Is it really smart and effective for me, for example, to look for a counter trade here? Or is it more likely since I can see from the volume here that there's real pressure that the thing will keep falling?"

### R10. Order book last: pace, absorption, fake orders (SOURCED)

Rule: Last step: see whether the market is played fast or lethargic, absorbed, or with fake large orders.

### R11. Where the big money sits comfortably (SOURCED)

Rule: If the big money is comfortably in the middle and content, little will happen.

> "is the really big money in particular currently situated somewhere comfortably in the middle and quite content with its present position? then you can simply assume that not much is going to happen"
