## Rules

### R1. Stop hunting: tiny volume under the lows (SOURCED)

Rule: Under the lows the footprint often shows single-digit trades, so there is little for big players to hunt; nobody but the broker knows where stops sit or how much liquidity is behind them.

> "you can often see quite clearly that sometimes trades are happening there in the singledigit range."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | BEGIN**

### R2. Big players build slowly (SOURCED)

Rule: Large positions are built over time, via spreads and hedges, sometimes over weeks, not in a minute.

> "It happens over time depending on the size and position they're building."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | BEGIN**

### R3. Big players prefer fair-priced ranges (SOURCED)

Rule: Large institutions need long to route size and prefer fairly priced sideways structures for it.

> "And when do they prefer to do this? When markets are fairly priced."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | BEGIN**

### R4. Forced closing at market (SOURCED)

Rule: A long side that runs out of money closes by selling at market; the market turns aggressive, a counter-move arises and new buyers come in.

> "Then some positions are closed and how do they usually do that? Often by selling at market because they realize there's nothing left."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | MIDDLE**

### R5. Trend phases need substance (SOURCED)

Rule: Aggressive fast trend phases are usually triggered by fundamental news; large players do not move a market for purely technical reasons, and where the new fair price is cannot be known in advance.

> "And large institutional players aren't interested in just briefly moving a market from A to B for technical reasons."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | MIDDLE**

### R6. Support is confirmed by volume (SOURCED)

Rule: A level becomes real support or resistance only when price reaches it, volume is generated, it holds and price moves away; levels are assumptions until the market confirms.

> "If I see for example that volume is being generated there, that level holds and the market moves away from it, then for me that's when it truly becomes real support and resistance because the market confirms it for me."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | MIDDLE**

### R7. Accepted area is revisited by the same participants (SOURCED)

Rule: Where volume formed and price accepted, expect the participants to be present on return; if they are gone it is no longer support or resistance.

> "then I expect that the next time the price comes back to that area, those participants will still be present."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | MIDDLE**

### R8. A large print is information, not intent (SOURCED)

Rule: Seeing 10,000 contracts bought shows something big happening, but it may be one player or many; use it for a few ticks and check how it is traded.

> "It could be just one big player, but it could also be 10,000 individual traders."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | MIDDLE**

### R9. Liquidity providers vanish when speed is too high (SOURCED)

Rule: Market makers and high-frequency firms leave when volatility passes a speed threshold, and the book can lose most of its normal volume.

> "all of the highfrequency trading firms, they are no longer present when the market exceeds a certain speed threshold."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | END**

### R10. Liquidity vanishes before scheduled events (SOURCED)

Rule: Book depth disappears before rate decisions and central bank speeches because participants do not want risk (index and rates events, not a BTC rule).

> "It just vanishes from the books."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | END**

### R11. Spike candle with little traded volume (SOURCED)

Rule: A huge candle may reflect thin liquidity getting filled, not a massive sell-off; little volume traded inside it, and price may return.

> "It just means that there was liquidity further down and it got filled there."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | END**

### R12. Follow the volume imbalance only with context (SOURCED)

Rule: Trading toward more volume is fine in context, but liquidity that is created and always rejected on the way up may mean someone is building a position.

> "If the market in a certain way creates liquidity but always rejects it and keeps moving higher and there are always such strong rejections, it could also mean that someone is perhaps building a position there."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | END**

### R13. Supply and demand dominate (SOURCED)

Rule: Supply and demand dominate whatever the player size.

> "supply and demand always dominate the market, no matter how big the players are."
**Source GPS: RwQBdF9TSvc | END**
