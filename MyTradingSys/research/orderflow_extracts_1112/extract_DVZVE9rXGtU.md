## Rules

### R1. Who is forced: slow institutional routing (SOURCED)

Rule: Big funds take days to route orders; the tape or book shows whether someone is building or closing a position.

> "When large institutional pension funds or similar entities take positions in the markets, it sometimes takes them several days to route their orders. And that works to our advantage as day traders."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | BEGIN**

### R2. Build vs close (SOURCED)

Rule: Large buying shows in time and sales or the book; one can infer opening versus closing.

> "You can draw conclusions from that, for example, whether someone is just closing a position or actually building one up."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | BEGIN**

### R3. Rhythmic execution (SOURCED)

Rule: Some markets show scheduled order placement every few minutes; recognising it is an edge.

> "maybe it's more likely that there's a rhythmic system behind it, which basically dictates that every few minutes or every 30 minutes a certain number of orders must be placed into the market."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | BEGIN**

### R4. Index trackers are mechanical (SOURCED)

Rule: Index trackers buy by weight in similar-sized orders and follow the market whichever way it runs (index-product mechanic, not a BTC rule).

> "similar orders of similar size keep coming into the market because those guys don't put in a lot of effort. They just have to position it on the market."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | BEGIN**

### R5. Forced seller: big order chasing price (SOURCED)

Rule: A huge order that keeps chasing price away signals someone must exit, often a margin call; not a buyer to follow.

> "But in most cases like this, it's usually because someone has gotten into trouble and now needs to exit the market."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | MIDDLE**

### R6. Margin call signature (SOURCED)

Rule: Large volume pushed close to market or at market price shows distress rather than patience.

> "sometimes even forcing big trades at market price. And then, you know, what needs to be done in that situation."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | MIDDLE**

### R7. Forced dumps are free money, but never stand in the way (SOURCED)

Rule: Forced position dumps are an opportunity once finished; while it runs, do not trade against it.

> "when some freaks are forced to dump their positions into the market, that's basically free money."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | MIDDLE**

### R8. Wait for the forced flow to end (SOURCED)

Rule: Do not fade a forced flow; wait until it is over.

> "there's just no point in going against it. You just have to wait until you can see that it's over."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | MIDDLE**

### R9. Crowd side at the hype (SOURCED)

Rule: Institutions sell into hype, retail buys at the peak (Tom's claim, not tested by us).

> "Usually, the institutional investors are selling at the hype, and the retail investors are buying at the hype."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | MIDDLE**

### R10. Leverage kills when big players move (SOURCED)

Rule: When big players must build positions, only ride along; leveraged wrong-way positions are wiped out.

> "if the big players start to move, if the big players have to start building positions, forget it."
**Source GPS: DVZVE9rXGtU | MIDDLE**
