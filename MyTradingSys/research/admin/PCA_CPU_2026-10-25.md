## Plain explanation
PcaSvc is the "Program Compatibility Assistant". It watches programs starting, applies compatibility fixes to old installers and shows the "this program might not have installed correctly" box, and sends compatibility telemetry. Our sessions start hundreds of short python/git/bash programs, so it wakes constantly.
Nothing in MTS, Java, Python, git, Claude or Defender depends on it. Repo references: only `scripts/operator/defender_exclusions.ps1` (`-StopPcaSvc`, with undo `-Remove -StopPcaSvc`), `cpu_cleanup.ps1:52` (lists it as stoppable) and `cpu_stress.py` (measures it). Risk of disabling: lose only those prompts/auto-fixes for old installers; reversible.

## Operator, elevated PowerShell (two lines) and undo
    Stop-Service PcaSvc -Force; Set-Service PcaSvc -StartupType Disabled
Undo: `Set-Service PcaSvc -StartupType Manual; Start-Service PcaSvc`
Existing script does the same: `scripts/operator/defender_exclusions.ps1 -StopPcaSvc` (undo `-Remove -StopPcaSvc`; note its undo sets Automatic, stock is Manual).
Policy alternative (`HKLM\SOFTWARE\Policies\Microsoft\Windows\AppCompat` DisablePCA=1): cleaner for fleet/GPO and survives feature updates that reset service start types, but needs a reboot/service restart to take effect and is less obvious to undo. For one notebook the service Disabled is simpler. CPU after: NOT_MEASURED (needs the elevated step); expected saving ~2.6% of box average, bursts up to one core.
