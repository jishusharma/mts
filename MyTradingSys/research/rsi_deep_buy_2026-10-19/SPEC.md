# RSI DEEP-BUY study: buy the RSI extreme in every timeframe, exit on RSI levels or divergence (spec, 2026-10-19)

Status: EXPLORATORY SPEC, nothing here is a trading rule. Written by the Supervisor (login C, JIS-NB) from the operator's words; the build and runs go to other machines and models (section 7).
Patched 2026-10-20 with SHOT_REVIEW.md (c) C1-C9 (Steps G, H, I, J, section 3, trial ledger in section 5); what was applied, adjusted or skipped: APPLIED.md.
Public repo: no runtime/order/account data. Fee-net out-of-sample results decide, never doctrine (PROJECT_VISION).

## 0. Operator request (SOURCED, operator 2026-10-19, chat)
1. First find, per timeframe (1m, 2m, 3m, 5m and likewise higher), how low RSI goes in the past ("how negative it goes").
2. Blindly buy when RSI reaches those lows; sell when RSI hits 40 in each timeframe.
3. Iterate with variants from Malkan's 40/50/60 loop: stay in while RSI enters 40, takes support on its MA, crosses 50, takes support on 50, heads to 60; leave on divergence (test 1m, 2m, 3m ... which TF's divergence exit pays best).
4. No stop loss. Price can keep falling after RSI turns up from its lowest point; the position may be added to (double, triple) as price falls. Test these permutations.
5. Hit-and-trial research, channelled properly; run it on different models.
Not required to follow Malkan's rules (OPERATOR); his 40/50/60 loop is a source for variants. Malkan quotes live in `extracts/processed/malkan/` and `research/rsi_2a_2026-10-09/RSI_2A_SPEC.md` (T3: Indian equities D/W/M, not crypto, not intraday: a transfer assumption, say so).

## 2. Phase 1: the RSI depth ladder and its probabilities (operator refinement 2026-10-20, replaces the first draft of this section)
Operator words (SOURCED, chat 2026-10-20): record the lowest RSI reading per period, then the second lowest, third, fourth, fifth; note which levels are visited once and which more than once; from that get the real probability of each level being hit; the 40 line is visited often, 20 and 80/90 rarely; define a "not frequent" zone (the line is a backtest parameter, adjusted during testing); inside it measure how much further RSI goes, then what price does next (e.g. "RSI at 22: RSI bounces 80% of the time, price 50%, by this many percent"); everything in probabilities; then trade on them. Open mind: say which values are fixed and which are varied.

Per RSI timeframe t in {1m, 2m, 3m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 4h, 1d} (RSI14 on close, resampled from 1m, UTC) and per anchor window W in {1h, 4h, 1d, 1w} (non-overlapping, UTC-aligned; only windows longer than t; the operator's example is t=1m with W=1h, 4h, 1d):
- Step A, ladder of troughs. In each window find RSI troughs (local minimum within +-k bars; k in {3, 5, 10}), rank them: rank1 = lowest, rank2 = second lowest, ... up to rank5. Record value, time, price, and the visit count of every integer level L (5..50): a VISIT = RSI closes at or below L after having been at least h points above L (hysteresis h in {2, 3, 5}, so noise around the line is one visit, not twenty). Output `ladder_<t>_<W>.csv` (one row per window) and `ladder_summary.csv`.
- Step B, touch probability. For each (t, W, L): P_touch = share of windows whose minimum is at or below L; mean visits given touched; P(2 or more visits | touched). This is the curve that says 40 is common and 20 is rare.
- Step C, the "not frequent" zone. Rare zone for (t, W) = levels with P_touch below q, q in {20%, 10%, 5%, 2%} (OURS, a parameter, tuned on DEV). Report each (t, W) boundary L*(q).
- Step D, how deep does it go once inside. For each level L and each visit: P(RSI reaches L-d later in the same window | it reached L now), d in {1, 2, 3, 5}, also conditioned on visit number (1st, 2nd, 3rd visit of L in this window) and on how much of the window has elapsed. Only information known at the touch time may condition the estimate: "this will be the window's lowest" is not usable (that is look-ahead); "RSI just touched L, it is the n-th visit, p percent of the window is gone" is.
- Step E, what happens next (the tradeable probabilities). Event = a visit to level L (first down-cross after hysteresis). From the next bar's open measure: P(RSI bounce) = RSI rises b points (b in {3, 5, 10}) from its running minimum before it falls a further d points; P(price bounce) = price higher than the entry fill by more than the 0.10% round-trip fee after H (H in {1, 5, 15, 60} bars of t and fixed 15m/1h/4h); distribution of the price bounce size (p10/p25/median/p75/p90) and of the adverse move before it (MAE). Each as a table by (t, W, L, visit number), with Wilson 95% intervals and effective-N counted in independent DAYS (events cluster).
- Step F, CONTEXT (operator 2026-10-20: how far RSI goes depends on the phase; a level untouched for years or for a whole week changes where the reaction happens). Every table of Steps B-E is built three ways and compared:
  - F0 UNCONDITIONAL: all history before the event (expanding window). The baseline.
  - F1 MACRO PHASE: a mechanical label at the event bar from data strictly before it. OURS definitions (the operator's words trending / consolidating / accumulation / distribution have no formula in the sources, so these are proposals to be tuned, each one a trial): trend = sign and size of the 30d and 90d return plus price vs its 200d average; consolidation = 30d range width below its own 1y percentile (and ADX low); accumulation = consolidation entered after a drawdown from the 1y high above x%; distribution = consolidation entered after a run-up; plus volatility tercile. Max 3 label schemes, max 6 states each (pre-registered, no more).
  - F2 RECENT CONTEXT (rolling, per RSI timeframe t): look back L in {4h, 6h, 12h, 1d, 3d, 1w}. Features computed on the trailing window only: trailing floor = lowest RSI reached in L; touches of each level in L; trailing bounce record = of the visits in L to each level, the share where RSI bounced b points and where price bounced past the fee, and the typical bounce size. The operator's example: if RSI never reached 30 in the last week, the working floor is about 31, so the reaction table for 31, 32, 33 in that week is the relevant one, and a reading of 25 is a new regime (rarer than anything in L). Event coordinates are therefore BOTH absolute (RSI = 22) and relative (RSI minus trailing floor in L, and its rank among the visits in L).
  - Rolling vs full history for rarity: P_touch(L) is estimated on expanding history, on the last 1y, 90d and 30d; the report shows which memory length predicts the next window's touches best (calibration), because "10 was last seen years ago" and "30 was not touched this week" are different statements.
  - Entry is not limited to the extreme: tables are by level bin (1-point bins) so entries at 11, 12, 13 or 20 are measured too, and the all-time-low touch is one more bin, not a special rule.
  - Guard against overfitting: context multiplies the cell count, so (1) all context features are causal (data before the event bar only; a test plants a future bar and requires unchanged output), (2) estimates use shrinkage toward the parent cell (context cell shrunk to the unconditional cell by empirical Bayes), (3) a context cell needs at least 30 independent days, (4) Holm over the whole pre-registered grid, (5) walk-forward: probabilities for each event use only past events, so VALIDATION is a true forward test of the full pipeline, (6) a context only counts if it beats F0 in calibration and in net EV on VALIDATION, otherwise F0 is the answer.
- Step G, LEVEL-TRANSITION RACE (operator 2026-10-20: "RSI enters 28 and 26 this frequently, and what happens from there: bounces up or continues down, in probabilities"). This is the operator's core table and it is a first-passage problem on the RSI path. Per RSI timeframe t, level grid L (step 2 points, 40 down to 6; step 1 as sensitivity), event = ENTRY to L (RSI closes at or below L after being at least h=3 above it; known at the bar close, fill next open):
  - Frequency: entries to L per 100 days, share of entry-days, and mean entries per day given at least one (the once-vs-more-than-once question), per year and per macro/recent class of Step F.
  - Race: from the entry bar, which comes first within N bars (N = 60 bars of t, and 4 hours as a time-based sensitivity): DOWN = RSI closes at or below L-s (s in {2, 4}), or UP = RSI closes at or above L+b (b in {3, 5}) measured from the ENTRY level. Report P(DOWN first), P(UP first), P(neither by N), median bars to resolve. Second race from the running minimum: after the entry, P(RSI bounces b points off its running low before making a new low of at least s points) (the "bounce" vs "still continues down" split).
  - Path chain: P(reach L-2 | entered L), P(reach L-4 | entered L and L-2), ... so a path such as 28 -> 26 -> 24 has an explicit probability and the table shows where the chain thins (the rare zone appears as the place where the conditional probabilities stop being informative or the counts fall under 30 independent days). Condition also on the path so far (number of levels already visited in this episode, bars since the episode started).
  - Price branch: for every RSI branch above, the price result from the fill: P(price above fill + 0.10% fee within H), bounce size quantiles, further drop quantiles (MAE), both in % and in ATR units, because RSI points and price percent are different scales (OPUS_FRAMING s2.1). RSI "bounce" alone is nearly mechanical; the price columns carry the verdict.
  - Output `race_<t>.csv` (one row per level and parameter set) with Wilson 95% intervals and effective N in independent days, plus a short readable ladder in the report: "RSI at 28: enters X/100 days; next 60 bars: A% continue down to 26, B% bounce to 31, C% neither; price above fee in D%, median +E%, median further drop F%".
  - Mirror (SHOT_REVIEW C8, adjusted to OPUS_FRAMING s2.3/s5): every Step G-J table is also built for RSI highs (short bias) with the same grid, reported after the long side. 5 of 11 operator marks were bearish (SHOT_REVIEW (b)10). Built only for the TFs that survive Stage 0 on the long side, its rows are exploratory in the trial ledger, and it does not block the engine (OPUS_FRAMING stage order).
- Step H, LAYER CONFLUENCE (operator 2026-10-20: an RSI low sets a LONG bias; at that moment take the other layers' reading on the same timeframe and the support from higher timeframes; "think intelligently how to play with this"). Design:
  - Bias vector. At each Step G entry event, collect for every available layer family a causal vote in {+1 long, 0 neutral/no-reading, -1 short} on the SAME TF and on the next two TFs up (higher-TF value = last COMPLETED bar only; OPUS_FRAMING s2.2). Candidate voters (each only if it has a historical, bar-by-bar, look-ahead-clean output on the BTC tape; else NOT_IN_DATA, never zero-filled): the same RSI family on a higher TF (RSI state bands <30 / 30-40 / >40, RSI turning up, Malkan 40/60 reclaim `rsi_reclaim`), Kurisko quad stochastics (turn from oversold), Bollinger position (below lower band, `guidance` BB), VWAP/session levels (`session_levels`: prior day/week low reclaimed or swept), trendline/pitchfork support touch, wavetrend cross, turn-of-candle, CryptoCred setups, positioning (funding, taker-buy share/CVD proxy from the 1m taker columns; OI only forward-collected), Tom/Fabio order-flow layers only where a historical encoding exists (Tom core BTC encoding is STOPPED; use only what `layer_map.json` marks usable); opposite-side divergence on the next TF up, stamped at its confirmation and still within its last 20 bars (-1 for a long), and the same-side one (+1) (window 20 bars OURS; SHOT_REVIEW 34a949-3, the only live operator call, failed against a 5m bullish divergence 30 min earlier); oscillator agreement count (RSI, StochRSI, WaveTrend divergence at the same pivots, 0-3), only once the WaveTrend inputs are pinned (card `wavetrend-data-inference`; SHOT_REVIEW (b)9: WT failed to confirm twice and price still bounced, so agreement is a feature, not a rule). Both new voters stay inside the 8-voter cap below. A layer that cannot be replayed causally on history is excluded from the study and listed.
  - Votes are defined BEFORE any outcome is read (pre-registered table: layer, TF, rule, +1/-1 condition), one rule per layer, no tuning of the votes on outcomes. Standalone layer results are mostly "no edge" (LAYERS.md), so a layer is here a FEATURE of the state, not a signal.
  - Confluence score = (number of +1 votes) - (number of -1 votes), split into same-TF score and higher-TF score; plus the count of layers actually reporting (coverage). Compare equal-weight counts first; weighted or learned combinations only after the count version is measured, with walk-forward fitting and a hard cap on parameters.
  - Questions answered, each as a table over the Step G race: (1) does P(price bounce above fee) and P(continue down) change monotonically with the same-TF score and with the higher-TF score? (2) which single layer moves the odds most (add-one and drop-one), (3) does higher-TF alignment matter more than same-TF (buying a 1m low when the 1h and 4h also show oversold/turning = trend continuation vs a pullback in neutral higher TF; report both, do not assume the direction), (4) is there a score above which the rare zone becomes tradeable that the RSI level alone does not give, (5) the same for the mirror: highs set a short bias.
  - Guards: fixed small voter set (max 8 voters, max 3 TFs), score levels pooled to 4 buckets, independent-day counts, Holm over the pre-registered table, VALIDATION read once, and "beats RSI-level-only on log-loss and fee-net EV per day" or it is dropped. A confluence that only works on the days several layers fire together is a regime detector: report it next to the macro label of Step F.
- Step I, DIVERGENCE + SPEED/VOLATILITY CONTEXT (operator 2026-10-20: RSI makes a higher low while price makes a lower low; buy on it; measure how far price moves in %; the answer depends on what happened over the last 4h/12h/24h/week, volatility, ATR and the speed of the decline).
  - Causal divergence event. Bullish divergence = price makes a lower swing low than the previous swing low while RSI makes a higher swing low, with the first RSI low below level L (Step G grid). The event stamps at the bar where the second RSI low is CONFIRMED (RSI up b=3 points off it, or k bars after), never at the low itself; fill at the next open. Use `rsi_divergence.py` only after a test shows its stamp is not earlier than the confirming bar (OPUS_FRAMING s2.2 item 5). Sequence features (SHOT_REVIEW C3): firing number against the same anchor (1st, 2nd, 3rd ... qualifying B since the anchor was set, Step J), firing number in the decline (anchor replacements counted), and whether earlier firings reached +fee before a lower low (only firings whose result was known before this B's stamp; unresolved ones are a separate category, so no look-ahead). Every firing is an event; the study never keeps only the last one (the remembered one is hindsight: SHOT_REVIEW (b)3, 30m 10-08 fired 9 times against one anchor, 8 weak or losing at 4 h).
  - Control. The comparison that matters is divergence vs NO divergence at the same RSI level, same TF, same trailing volatility (matched bars). Prior work: divergence as a standalone entry is fee-net negative on every TF (rsi_divergence_mtf REPORT.md), so this step tests whether CONTEXT rescues it, not whether it works alone.
  - Outcome: forward price move in % and in ATR units, MFE/MAE, P(price above fill + 0.10% fee within H), P(new lower low within N bars), as in Step G.
  - Context vector (fixed list, computed on completed bars only; max 8 features, pre-registered): (1) returns over 1h/4h/12h/24h/7d in ATR units; (2) drawdown from the trailing high and hours since that high, and whether B's low is a new trailing-24h low and by how much in ATR (sweep; 24h OURS; SHOT_REVIEW (b)8, merged into (2) to keep the 8-feature cap); (3) decline speed = ATR units per hour since the high; (4) speed change = last 1h speed vs the prior 4h speed (accelerating vs decelerating, the time-and-price read); (5) bar-size trend on a mid TF (5m range z-score and its slope: are candles getting bigger); (6) realised volatility percentile (trailing 30d) and ATR(14) on t and on 1h; (7) volume / taker-buy share z-score; (8) session (Asia/EU/US). Momentum indicators already in the repo (Kurisko, wavetrend, higher-TF RSI) enter through the Step H vote table, not as extra free features.
  - Model: first cross-tabs on 3 buckets per feature, then at most one shallow tree or regularised logistic with at most 6 features, fitted walk-forward (each event sees only earlier events), reported against the Step G level-only table and against the Step H confluence score. Keep only what beats them on VALIDATION log-loss and fee-net EV per day.
- Step J, DIVERGENCE PAIRING, RSI REGIME AND REVERSAL SCALES (operator 2026-10-20, late: lows at RSI 20, then 30, then 25 -> 25 vs 20 is still a divergence if price is lower; in a strong uptrend RSI rarely goes under 40 and snaps back in 1-2 tries, so a 30/20 reading means the context changed; reversals exist at short, mid and long scale and inside ranges; the US-open range is bigger even on 1m; "phase" = everything that happened inside some cycle or period, session-wise or otherwise).
  - Pairing rule. When swing lows are L1 (RSI 20), L2 (RSI 30), L3 (RSI 25), the pair that defines "divergence" is a parameter, each a trial: P-adj (compare with the previous swing low only: 25 vs 30 = no divergence if price is lower), P-anchor (compare with the lowest RSI low in the episode/lookback: 25 vs 20 = divergence if price at L3 is lower than price at L1), P-any (any earlier low in the lookback shows a higher RSI with lower price). The price condition is always checked, not assumed. Record divergence multiplicity (how many earlier lows L3 diverges from) and the chain (L1 -> L3 skipping L2) as features; report P(bounce) and the price move per pairing rule and per multiplicity. Primary rule = P-anchor (SHOT_REVIEW C1; operator evidence: in 11 marked pairs the first point is the RSI extreme of the leg, P-adj misses 4; SHOT_REVIEW (b)1). Anchor = the lowest RSI close pivot since the episode start; the episode starts when RSI closes at or below the Step G entry level L after being above L+h (h = 3, fixed, OPUS_FRAMING s1), and ends when RSI closes above 50 (OURS; 40 and 60 as trials; the choice changes the event set, SHOT_REVIEW (b)2). A new lower RSI pivot inside the episode replaces the anchor. P-adj and P-any are reported as variants, plus P-anchor-split (RSI anchor = RSI extreme, price reference = any earlier price swing inside the episode; the operator draws the two panels between different bars, SHOT_REVIEW Review).
  - Price basis and tolerance (SHOT_REVIEW C2). Each pairing rule is run with price = close (primary, SOURCED Malkan close-to-close: "Close to close. Because RSI is based on closing price.", `rsi_divergence.py:3`) and price = wick low/high (variant, OURS). 'Lower low' = B below A by more than -eps (B < A + eps), eps in {0 (primary), 0.05%, 0.1 ATR(t)} (OURS), so equal lows count when eps > 0. A lower high on price with a lower RSI high (or the long mirror: higher low on price with a higher RSI low) is labelled FAILURE_SWING, never divergence, and gets its own row. min RSI difference in {0 (primary, SOURCED Malkan "decimals count", `rsi_divergence.py:7`), 2, 5} (2 and 5 OURS) as trials. First pass runs the primary row only (P-anchor, end 50, close, eps 0, min diff 0); the other values are exploratory trial-ledger rows (OPUS_FRAMING s2.3).
  - RSI regime of the TF (Cardwell/Malkan style range shift, SOURCED only where the Malkan extract says so, else OURS): trailing RSI floor and ceiling over a lookback (30d for 1m-1h; 1y for 4h-1d), share of bars in <30, 30-40, 40-60, 60-70, >70, count of failed dips under 40 that returned within 1-3 attempts. ## 3. Phase 2: exits and position handling
Entry sizes in units of u (OURS: u = 1 unit of quote notional, spot-style 1x, no leverage in the headline arm; a 3x perp arm is a sensitivity only).
Exit arms (applied per TF with the same TF's RSI unless stated):
- X1: RSI(TF) crosses up through 40 (the operator's rule).
- X2: RSI crosses 50. X3: RSI crosses 60. Per TF report the gross move from the RSI trough close to the first close at 40/50/60 in % and in fee multiples (SHOT_REVIEW C9; table produced by `rsi-deep-econ-floor`, C12); on 1m X1 is about 2.6 fees (one example, SHOT_REVIEW ddb38-1: +0.26% / +0.44% / +0.58% to 40/50/60), so 1m X1 is expected to die at H1.
- X4 (Malkan loop): hold while RSI keeps the loop (after crossing 40 it stays above its own 40 line on closes, or above its RSI MA); leave when the loop breaks, i.e. RSI closes back below 40 after having crossed 50 (OURS reading of the loop; quote it from the extract where possible, else tag OURS).
- X5: bearish divergence on divergence-TF D in {1m, 2m, 3m, 5m, 15m} (use `rsi_divergence.py`, Malkan min 3 bars, close-to-close) after the entry; D is a parameter, the entry TF is separate.
- X6: time stop N bars (control arm, OURS).
Scale-in ladders (no stop; every add is a new unit at the next open):
- S0: no adds. S1: add 1 unit each time price falls a further a% below the last fill (a in {0.5, 1, 2}% scaled by the TF's median bar range; OURS). S2: double each add (1, 2, 4). S3: add on each new RSI low below the previous trough (the "lower low" case). Max adds M in {2, 4, 6}; after M the position just waits (no stop) or hits the ruin test.
- Exit applies to the whole stack at its average cost; fees on every fill.

## 4. Metrics (a no-stop strategy is judged on the account, not the hit rate)
Per arm and TF: fee-net return per trade and per DAY (days, not events, are the unit: events cluster; overlapping stacks are one episode), Holm-adjusted across the grid, realised MDE vs the effect (repo convention). Plus: max capital deployed, max adverse excursion in % and in units of u, longest underwater stretch (days), share of episodes still open at data end (reported separately, never dropped, mark at last price), equity curve with unrealised PnL, max drawdown, worst-day loss. RUIN TEST: replay 2022-11 (FTX), 2020-03 style and the 2024-08-05 flush: capital needed to survive with M adds and no stop; a ladder that needs more than the stated bankroll is rejected whatever its mean return. Funding for the perp arm taken from real funding files (no zero-fill; missing = NOT_IN_DATA).
Verdict thresholds (OURS, frozen before any outcome): fee-net mean per day above 0 on DEV and VALIDATION, same sign in more than half the years, t >= 2 after Holm, ruin test passed, at least 100 independent days of episodes.

## 5. Splits, trials, honesty
BTCUSDT 1m 2022-01-01 .. data end. DEV = 2022-01-01 .. 2024-06-30 (matches the layer lab guard `dev_split_guard`), VALIDATION = 2024-07-01 .. 2025-06-30, HOLDOUT after, read ONCE.
