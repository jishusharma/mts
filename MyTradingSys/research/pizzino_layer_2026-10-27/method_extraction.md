## Definitions (carried from the two files)

- **Michael 1/2/3-bar (SOURCED):** mechanical trend state (up / down / neutral) per nested time frame; 1-bar low-term, 2-bar mid-term, 3-bar high-term; written "1-day 1-bar". Confluence = all three agree = strong bias; mixed = indecision. Lead-lag: 1-bar (and 2-bar) turn first, "always a precursor" for the next time frame.
- **Michael trigger level (SOURCED):** price at which the 1-bar trend flips (61,200), next pivot as target (63,000); higher-time-frame strength needs closes above the pivot (weekly closes, higher lows).
- **Jason three-bar signal (SOURCED):** three consecutive same-direction bars (higher highs and higher lows after a low; lower highs and lower lows after a high) from a significant turning point ("generally all-time high, but not always"); on D, 3D, W; short term; small versions "relatively unconvincing".
- **50% level (SOURCED, both):** midpoint of the prior major run (top and bottom halved). Above = strong half, below = weak half. Hold above after a pullback = strength; rejection off it = correction continues; a change of trend beneath it (Michael) is the lead for weakness; retest from underneath after a breakdown = weak.
- **Pivot point (SOURCED, Michael, loose):** a named price level (83,000; 84,300; 88; 63,000); breakdown beneath it is the early lead; confluence when it sits on the 50% level.
- **Retest (SOURCED, loose, both):** price returns to a broken level or the 50%; hold (consecutive closes beyond) = continuation, rejection = reversal.
- **Confluence of levels (SOURCED, Jason):** 50% stacked with moving averages and swing tops (BTC 92k = 50% + 100-week near 90k + 98k monthly swing top).
