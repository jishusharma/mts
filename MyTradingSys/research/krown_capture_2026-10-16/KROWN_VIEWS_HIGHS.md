## 4. 'Tick Below' / 'Tick Above' (Precision, Stops, and Invalidation)

> "Invalidation. The higher of the two tops, the tick above and the sequence never broke."
**Source GPS: fMRvOt26xcA | BEGIN**

> "Your stop gets hit at the exact low, like literally the tick, the tick of the low. Then of course, what happens? Price action reverses and runs without you, naturally."
**Source GPS: dzH7m9cQeZA | BEGIN**

*Note on exact phrase `tick below`:* In Krown's canonical corpus, Eric references exact execution at the margin as "the tick, the tick of the low" and upside invalidation as "the tick above". Trader John Kurisko (`PjigwAmhiT0`) explicitly uses the phrase "one or two ticks below the low candlestick", while Krown uses "the tick of the low" for stops and "the tick above" for sequence invalidation.

## 5. Daily Close Above / Below a Level (Verdict vs Rumor)

**Source GPS: _t_XIBrcjRk | MIDDLE**

> "Price needs to close above the previous dominant high, which is right here. It gets close there, but close but no cigar is important. As long as you're below there, I would not validate a reversal."

## 6. What a Broken vs Unbroken High / Level Means (Close vs Wick)

> "And one question at every broken level is did it close and hold or did it stab and come home?"
