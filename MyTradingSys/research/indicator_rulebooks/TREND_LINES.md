## 2. Developer's own rules (SOURCED)
- **Foundational Authors:** Robert D. Edwards and John Magee, *Technical Analysis of Stock Trends* (1948) [SOURCED: EDW]; John J. Murphy, *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets* (1999, Chapter 3 "Basic Concepts of Trend") [SOURCED: MUR].
- **Core Stated Rules:**
  1. *Two Points Propose, Three Points Validate:* A trend line requires at least two swing points to be drawn (tentative hypothesis), but is not officially confirmed or endowed with "authority" until price tests and respects the line on a third distinct reaction [SOURCED: MUR; EDW].
  2. *Uptrend vs Downtrend Geometry:* An uptrend line connects successive higher reaction lows (support); a downtrend line connects successive lower reaction peaks (resistance) [SOURCED: EDW; MUR].
  3. *Slope and the 45-Degree Guideline:* A sustainable major trend line tends to advance at roughly a 45-degree angle. Extremely steep lines represent unsustainable momentum that will break from time alone without signaling immediate trend reversal; excessively flat lines indicate sluggish participation [SOURCED: MUR].
  4. *Logarithmic vs Linear Scaling:* For long-term or multi-cycle charts, ratio/logarithmic scaling is strongly recommended so that equal vertical distances represent identical percentage changes, preserving geometric trend consistency across wide price ranges [SOURCED: EDW].
  5. *Penetrations and Polarity Flip:* Minor intraday wick penetrations are often noise; valid breaks require a decisive closing price beyond the line (or filters like a 1–3% penetration or 2-day close rule). Decisively broken support lines flip to future overhead resistance (polarity change) [SOURCED: MUR; EDW].
  6. *Fan Principle:* When a trend line breaks, price frequently forms secondary and tertiary flatter trend lines; a decisive break of the third fan line signals definitive primary trend reversal [SOURCED: EDW].

## 7. Confluence role
**LEVEL**
- What it adds confidence to: Trend lines define dynamic support and resistance boundary locations and compression geometry (e.g. wedges, triangles, flag channels). They provide spatial framing for potential pullback entries (first return / retest) or breakout timing, and serve as an invalidation boundary (stop placement beyond the line).
- Rule: A trend line is never a standalone entry trigger. It serves as a **dynamic level filter** requiring confluence with horizontal structural support/resistance, volume profile acceptance, or microstructural candlestick confirmation (e.g. Malkan divergence break or Krown volume-anchor reaction) before taking execution risk.
