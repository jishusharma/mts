## TOP 5
1. **Hedging as the primary driver of futures imbalance (`gFJBg8Bsw68`, `cw8LWQmec1E`, `aursfDVYzUk`, `m3IMdc7QwN4`, `jXR1afMy-3o`)**: Tom states retail/speculators are only 1.5-2% (or 2-3% in `DVZVE9rXGtU`) of futures volume, while the rest is commercial/institutional hedging. When price leaves balance, hedgers are forced to buy/sell futures regardless of price, creating trend/imbalance moves.
2. **Forced hedger liquidation & counter-trend reversals (`cw8LWQmec1E`, `6BAzqewtPUk`, `k0zm5NYn0IQ`, `T6hDJcHrVBM`)**: Because hedgers enter at poor prices under urgency, if an aggressive player pushes price back through their entry or below key levels, hedgers are forced to liquidate abruptly, fueling sharp reversals/exaggerations that Tom trades as counter-trend setups.
3. **VIX > 20 regime & VIX derivatives (`C9n_d91Uitc`, `15JoLK0PbMU`)**: Above VIX 20, large investors stay out or hedge portfolios using VIX options/futures.
4. **No mean reversion when producers hedge (`4VXpUjhKG2U`, `96iPFDpXEcU`)**: When commodity producers hedge aggressively or markets hit limit-up/down, price gaps and Bollinger Band breakouts do not mean-revert the same day.
5. **Options/spread hedging & IV risk premium (`4P5WAxyPCis`, `RwQBdF9TSvc`, `StaphlRH8NQ`)**: Institutions scalp to delta-hedge options (`4P5WAxyPCis`), hedge equity/futures positions with spreads/options (`RwQBdF9TSvc`), and overpay for implied volatility to hedge downside (`StaphlRH8NQ`).
