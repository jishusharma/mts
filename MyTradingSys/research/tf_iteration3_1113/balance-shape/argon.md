## TOP 5

1. **D-shape is balance in Tom's rules, but `TREND_DOWN` in our code**: Tom (`jXR1afMy-3o`) defines the D setup as balance where continuations stop, whereas `MyTradingSys/runtime/regime_classifier.py:32` maps `vp_shape == "D"` to a `down += 1` vote.
2. **Bell-curve 60–70% balance rule is uncoded**: Tom (`BB3c8visnkI`) states 60–70% of the time markets form balance equilibrium bell-curve profiles; no code models bell-curve equilibrium.
3. **Double distribution + single prints**: Both Tom (`K8qtT2_axPo`, `-dMWA_bbm3o`) and Fabio (`Pz8f0wWW12M`) use double distributions (two profiles separated by single prints / thin low) to read institutional buying/selling; completely absent from our codebase.
4. **Closing-price count**: Tom (`jXR1afMy-3o`, `m3IMdc7QwN4`, `rrHj24GSR6M`) uses a closing-price count to narrow fair value and detect when trend continuations stop; not implemented anywhere in `MyTradingSys`.
5. **Weekly VAH/VAL balance regime**: Tom (`uFxYcpiaOpw`) uses the previous week's VAH and VAL to classify today as inside-balance (sideways) vs outside-balance (trending); our code has no weekly VAH/VAL regime check.

## SINGLE BIGGEST HOLE

`MyTradingSys/runtime/regime_classifier.py:31-32` reduces profile shape to a binary `P` (+1 up) vs `D` (+1 down) vote, inverting Tom's D-setup (balance/sideways) and omitting bell-curve equilibrium (60–70%), double distributions with single prints, weekly VAH/VAL balance, and the closing-price count.
