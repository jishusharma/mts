## Why an 80% win rate can lose money (plain words)

1. A trade that takes a small profit fast and gives a big stop room wins most of the time: aim at +0.3% with a -3% stop and the price reaches +0.3% first about 9 times in 10, even on a random entry.
2. But the one loss in ten costs 3%, ten times a win, so the 9 wins (+2.7%) are cancelled by one loss (-3%) before fees.
3. Every trade also pays about 0.22% in fees and slippage; that alone is as big as three-quarters of a 0.3% win.
4. So win rate only tells you how often you are right, not how much you make: what counts is win rate against the shape (breakeven win rate column in the shape table).
5. A list of signals showing 56-86% wins can be produced by this shape from a coin flip; ask for the average win, the average loss and the fee-net result before trusting it.

## Limits
Funding not charged. Maker variant assumes the limit fills when the next bar trades through it (queue position ignored; real fills are fewer and adverse-selected, so the maker numbers are optimistic). Stop-first on a same-bar touch is conservative; a 5m bar hides the true order. S3 OI is a point-in-time snapshot up to 1 h stale. Trade pooling across 20 correlated coins is handled by clustering the bootstrap on UTC entry day. Fee-net uses 22 bp (taker) / 13 bp (maker): the grid is not re-run for other fee levels.
