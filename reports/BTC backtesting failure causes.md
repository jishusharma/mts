# How noise, costs and regimes sink BTC backtests

Backtests fail mostly because of how strategies get chosen, not because markets suddenly change. When many configurations are tried and the best one is kept, a high in-sample Sharpe ratio is the *expected* result even if no configuration has any edge: ten tries on one year of data produce an expected best Sharpe of about 1.57 from pure noise. Live evidence agrees. Bank "alternative beta" indices lost a median of roughly 72-73% of their backtest Sharpe once live, and across 888 retail algorithms the backtest Sharpe explained almost none of the live Sharpe. On top of that general problem, three things are worse in crypto than in most markets. The data has no single trusted source and is padded with fake volume and prints that happened on one venue only. Fees, perpetual funding and adverse fills roughly halve a BTC edge before slippage is even counted. And BTC's history holds only three or four independent cycles, with tail shapes and volatility levels that change between them. For our own BTC backtest, the most likely causes, in order, are: an unrecorded trial count on a short history; an execution model that assumes close-price or same-bar fills with taker costs and funding understated; and parameters with fixed price distances calibrated in one volatility regime. Each of these can be tested directly, and the final section lays out the order to do it.

## Picking the best of many backtests guarantees disappointment

The core mechanism is the expected-maximum effect. The estimated annual Sharpe ratio of a zero-skill strategy over *y* years is roughly normal with a standard deviation of 1/√y. Pick the best of *N* independent tries and its expected Sharpe rises at about √(2 ln N). Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu work through the numbers: with only **N = 10 configurations on one year of data, the expected best in-sample Sharpe is 1.57, while every strategy's true out-of-sample Sharpe is zero** ([Bailey et al. 2014, Notices of the AMS](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)). More frequent data does not help: when the true Sharpe is zero, sampling more often leaves the variance unchanged, and only more years shrink it. The same paper's Minimum Backtest Length result sets the budget: **with 5 years of data, no more than about 45 independent configurations can be tried before an in-sample Sharpe of 1 becomes the expected product of noise, and on 2 years just 7 configurations are enough** ([Bailey et al. 2014](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)). Applying that formula to a typical crypto research setup (this arithmetic is ours, not the paper's): 100 independent variants on 4 years of data give an expected best Sharpe of about 1.26 with zero true skill. Free parameters multiply fast. Seven binary choices make 128 configurations, with an expected maximum Sharpe above 2.6, and the authors stress that entry thresholds, stops, profit targets, holding periods and sizing rules all count as parameters ([Bailey et al. 2014](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)).

The research community converges on one fix: count the trials. Harvey and Liu show that a single-test p-value of 0.05 becomes **0.401 after ten tests**, and that the right haircut is nonlinear. Marginal strategies lose far more than 50% of their Sharpe, while very strong ones lose less ([Harvey & Liu 2015, JPM](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)). Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue that, given the hundreds of factors already tested, a new factor must clear **t > 3.0 rather than 2.0** ([NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). The Deflated Sharpe Ratio formalises the same idea. It raises the rejection threshold with the number of trials and the dispersion of their Sharpe ratios, and adjusts for skew and kurtosis. In the paper's own example, a Sharpe 2.5 strategy found on five years of daily data fails the 95% test once the trial count and the fat tails are taken into account ([Bailey & López de Prado 2014](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)). The Probability of Backtest Overfitting framework asks a related question: how often the configuration that ranks best in-sample falls below the median out-of-sample. In one illustration **every in-sample Sharpe was positive, about 78% of out-of-sample Sharpes were negative, and higher in-sample Sharpe went with *worse* out-of-sample results** ([Bailey et al., Journal of Computational Finance](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)).

That last point is the most important one for practitioners. When performance has "memory" (mean-reverting regimes, crowding, bubbles bursting), overfitting stops being merely useless and becomes harmful. Tuning hard to recent history then predicts *negative* live returns, not zero ([Bailey et al. 2014](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)). Live data backs this up. In the Quantopian study of 888 algorithms with at least six months out of sample, **backtest Sharpe predicted live Sharpe with R² below 0.025, and the more a strategy had been backtested, the larger its gap between in-sample and out-of-sample results** ([Quantpedia summary of Wiecki et al. 2016](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)). Among about 215 bank alternative-beta indices, **only 18 matched their backtest Sharpe live**, and the most complex products lost over 30 percentage points more ([mathinvestor.org summary of Suhonen et al.](https://mathinvestor.org/2017/08/backtest-overfitting-in-smart-beta-investments/); this rests on secondary sources). Holdout testing does not rescue the process. Harvey and Liu note that revising a strategy after it fails out of sample turns the holdout into in-sample data, and that "there is no true OOS that uses historical data" once the researcher has seen it ([Harvey & Liu 2015](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)). A holdout also ignores the trial count and is unreliable below about 1,000 observations ([Bailey et al., JCF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)).

## The data quietly leaks the future and drops the losers

The second general class of failure enters through the dataset rather than the code. Look-ahead bias seldom looks like an obvious peek. It arrives through vendor histories that were backfilled, restated or revised after the fact. Banz and Breen found that portfolio returns differ significantly depending on whether Compustat or a look-ahead-free database is used ([AFA JoF 41(4)](https://afajof.org/issue/volume-41-issue-4/)). Later work shows that Compustat's re-standardisation rewrites historical sales and earnings ([Lyle, Siano & Yohn 2024](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/ADP/Fall%202024/Lyle%20Siano%20Yohn%202024.pdf)). Even using today's index membership as the historical universe distorts returns by an estimated **0.1-0.9% a year** ([arXiv 2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07078)). Survivorship bias works the same way. It grows with sample length and can create anomalies outright: once missing Nasdaq delisting returns are corrected to about −55%, **the Nasdaq size effect disappears** ([SAFE Frankfurt CRSP description](https://datacenter.safe-frankfurt.de/documents/Data_Description_CRSP_Shumway_20250317.pdf)).

Non-stationarity explains the rest of the "it worked, then it stopped" pattern. Across 97 published return predictors, returns fall **26% out of sample and 58% after publication**. The authors treat the first figure as an upper bound on data mining and attribute the remaining 32 points to traders arbitraging the effect away ([McLean & Pontiff 2016](https://counterpointfunds.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/PredictabilityMcleanPontiff.pdf)). True regime change does the rest. Momentum crashed when losers rallied 163% against winners' 8% in March-May 2009 ([Daniel & Moskowitz, NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf)). The US stock-bond correlation flipped from +0.18 (1926-1999) to about −0.2 (2000-2022) and then back ([EUR Pure, Molenaar et al. 2024](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/empirical-evidence-on-the-stock-bond-correlation/)). Returns alone cannot tell apart noise, crowding and genuine regime change. And the start and end dates of a backtest are themselves hidden trials: a window that ends just before a crash adds to the effective N without anyone recording it.

## Crypto data has no golden source and much of its volume is fake

In crypto these data problems are bigger, and some have no counterpart in traditional markets. A 2025 *Management Science* audit of leading vendors found **widespread mislabelling, unstable asset identifiers and large disagreements between providers on prices, volumes and market caps** ([Schwenkler, Shah & Yang 2025](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00611); only the qualitative findings could be extracted). Candles are also revised after the fact as late trades are backfilled, so a candle downloaded today can differ from the one a live system saw at the time ([Talos on Coin Metrics candles](https://www.talos.com/insights/improvements-to-coin-metrics-candles-data)). Single venues print prices that never existed anywhere else. On Binance.US, BTC fell to **$8,200 for under a minute while other exchanges traded near $64,200** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/121657/binance-us-blames-bitcoin-flash-crash-to-8200-on-a-bug-in-a-clients-trading-algorithm)). On 10 October 2025 USDe traded at about $0.65 on Binance, whose oracle priced it from its own order book, while it held near $1 everywhere else ([21Shares](https://www.21shares.com//research/why-did-ethenas-stablecoin-remain-stable-onchain-but-depegged-on-binance)). With no consolidated tape, "the BTC price" is a modelling choice. Makarov and Schoar document cross-exchange gaps that persisted for weeks and arbitrage profits above $1B in a few months of 2017-2018 ([JFE 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:2:p:293-319)). So a backtest is only honest about fills on the venue whose prices it used.

Volume is the least trustworthy field in the dataset. Peer-reviewed tests on 29 exchanges put wash trading at **over 70% of reported volume on unregulated venues**, while regulated venues look like traditional markets ([Cong et al., NBER w30783](https://nber.org/papers/w30783)). Bitwise's 2019 SEC presentation put the fake or non-economic share of reported BTC volume near 95% ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitwise-calls-out-to-sec-95-of-bitcoin-trade-volume-is-fake-real-market-is-or)), though Bitwise had a commercial interest in ETF approval. Forbes estimated a 51% overstatement of BTC volume in 2022 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/more-than-50-of-reported-bitcoin-trading-volume-is-likely-to-be-fake-or-non-economic-report)). Regulators have alleged that Binance traded on its own platform through about 300 house accounts ([The Block on the CFTC complaint](https://www.theblock.co/post/223143/binance-details-from-cftc-complaint)). Any feature built on volume, whether volume breakouts, VWAP anchors, turnover filters or capacity rules, can therefore be learning exchange marketing rather than trading demand. That relationship also shifts as enforcement and ranking methods change. Survivorship is extreme at the coin level: across 3,904 coins from 2014 to 2021 the bias was **62% a year for equal-weighted portfolios** (0.93% value-weighted), and the size premium was overstated by half ([Ammann et al.](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/items/bd8eb8d2-428c-47d2-b8a6-cc319f252037)). A BTC-only strategy escapes the coin problem but not the venue problem. Mt. Gox and FTX histories are missing from today's exchange APIs, so a "Binance since 2017" backtest never has to survive a counterparty failure. Manipulation leaves patterns in the data that cannot be repeated: bots on Mt. Gox were associated with roughly 4% daily price rises in 2013 ([Gandal et al. slides](https://tylermoore.utulsa.edu/weis17pres.pdf)), and Tether-timed buying with BTC price jumps in 2017 ([Griffin & Shams, JF 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:75:y:2020:i:4:p:1913-1964)).

## Fees, funding and adverse fills halve a BTC edge before slippage

Execution is where crypto backtests most often flatter the strategy, and the bias runs one way. At Binance USD-M futures VIP 0 rates of **0.02% maker and 0.05% taker**, a round trip that crosses the spread both ways costs about 0.10% in fees alone, 2.5 times the cost of two maker fills ([Binance Futures fee FAQ](https://www.binance.com/support/faq/360033544231)). The most careful crypto-specific estimate comes from He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter's perpetual-versus-spot strategy on Binance BTC from 2020 to 2024. Adding retail-tier maker fees alone cut **Sharpe from 3.53 to 1.80 and annual return from 13.7% to 6.4%**. The share of time the strategy could be active fell from 100% to 20%, because only large deviations still cleared the cost hurdle. This is an upper bound: the study modelled no slippage and assumed maker fills ([He et al., arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v5)). The pattern matches traditional markets, where costs reduced profitability in all 23 anomalies studied and few strategies with monthly turnover above about 50% stayed significant after costs ([Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)).

Costs that build up while a position is held catch out backtests that only charge per trade. The perpetual funding formula's baseline of 0.01% per 8 hours annualises to **10.95%** ([Coin Metrics docs](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/market-data/market-data-overview/market-funding-rates)), and BTC/ETH futures carry has averaged **above 10% a year**, with spikes far higher ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)). Funding also changes sign from one regime to the next: one cross-exchange snapshot read −0.7% annualised in late September 2026 and +16% a month later ([DeFi Rate 2026-09-25](https://defirate.com/perp/snapshot/2026-09-25T1925/); [DeFi Rate 2026-10-24](https://defirate.com/perps/snapshot/2026-10-24T0041/)). A perp backtest run on spot candles, or without the funding series, overstates long-biased returns and understates short-biased ones. Crypto momentum earns most of its alpha on the short side, exactly where borrow and funding costs bite ([Fieberg, Liedtke & Zaremba 2024](https://open.icm.edu.pl/handle/123456789/25544)).

Fills are the third leak, and they are adverse, not random. Albers, Cucuringu, Howison and Shestopaloff placed millions of real orders on Binance and Bybit. They found a consistent disadvantage against the last order-book snapshot, driven by adverse selection: **profitable orders filled worse than expected while unprofitable ones met expectations, and marketable limit orders often failed to fill** ([Albers et al., Quantitative Finance 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v25y2025i6p919-947.html)). The gap grew with volatility and latency, which are the conditions under which most signals fire. Depth disappears in exactly those moments. Binance BTC depth within 0.1% fell from about **100 BTC to 1.2 BTC** during the October 2023 ETF rumour ([CoinDesk on Kaiko data](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/markets/2023/10/17/bitcoin-liquidity-on-binance-plummeted-during-the-etf-rumor-induced-market-rollercoaster-kaiko)), and BTC slippage on major US exchanges tripled within hours on 5 August 2024 ([Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/moving-markets-liquidity-and-large-sell-orders)). Filling at a bar's close therefore hides two errors that both favour the strategy: the close is not an executable price, and a signal computed on a bar's close cannot be filled at that same close.

## BTC offers few regimes, shifting tails and self-made crashes

BTC's statistical behaviour shifts in ways that break parameters calibrated on a single window. Markov-switching GARCH beats single-regime GARCH for BTC Value-at-Risk because the volatility dynamics themselves change between regimes ([Ardia, Bluteau & Rüede 2019](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3180830)). The tails change too. BTC's excess kurtosis was **3.46 in 2017-2019, lower than the S&P 500's, and 50.87 in 2020** ([Palomar, Portfolio Optimization](https://bookdown.org/palomar/portfoliooptimizationbook/2.3-non-gaussianity-asymmetry-and-heavy-tails.html)). Its second moments may not even converge, which makes sample volatility and Sharpe estimates unstable from window to window ([Dehouche 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00395)). The level of volatility has also fallen steadily: realised daily volatility dropped from **7.58% in 2012 to 2.24% in 2025** ([KuCoin citing K33](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-volatility-drops-to-2-24-in-2025-lower-than-nvidia)), a 3.4x compression. Fixed-percentage stops, take-profits and breakout thresholds therefore mean something different in every part of a full-history backtest. Low average volatility did not mean low tail risk either. The 2025 record-low volatility year also produced the record **$19B single-day liquidation on 10 October**, when top-of-book depth fell by more than 90% on key venues ([FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/ja-jp/japan/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity); [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/forced-liquidations-in-2025-surpass-150-billion/)). Volatility-scaled sizing raises leverage in calm periods, which places it at its largest just before cascades like that one.

Positioning in derivatives makes part of the price path endogenous. High carry predicts crashes, and arbitrageurs face "spikes in margins and liquidations amid drawdowns" ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)). Glassnode finds that liquidation bands on Hyperliquid line up with spikes in liquidations across exchanges, though it makes no "magnet" or stop-hunting claim ([Glassnode Research](https://research.glassnode.com/liquidation-heatmaps/)). Auto-deleveraging can force-close *winning* positions once insurance buffers run out ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)). A spot OHLC backtest records the cascade's wick but cannot see the fills, the funding, the ADL or the fragile price levels behind it.

Edges that existed while the market was immature have decayed as institutions arrived. Time-series momentum across more than 3,100 coins "seems to have disappeared" after CME and CBOE futures launched in December 2017 ([Tinbergen seminar, Swinkels 2018](https://utilities.tinbergen.nl/?p=66235)); this is a seminar abstract that has not been replicated, and a 2020-2025 study finds momentum still meaningful in major coins ([VU journals](https://www.journals.vu.lt/BATP/en/article/view/44540)). The carry trade posted in-sample Sharpe ratios of about **7-10** ([CMU, Christin et al. 2022](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/azj/files/CarryTrade.v1.0.pdf)). Through much of 2025 it earned little or nothing over T-bills, and it unwound into the October sell-off ([Kaiko/Amberdata](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/the-carry-trade-that-broke-how-basis-compression-triggered-the-cascade)). Underneath all of this sits a sample-size problem. BTC has only three complete halving cycles, and even a pro-cycle paper concedes its evidence is "descriptive and economic rather than statistically significant per epoch" ([arXiv 2607.26188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26188)). Crypto momentum results reverse depending on the sample window ([Springer FMPM 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). For any regime-dependent edge, the effective sample size is the number of regimes, about three or four cycles and two or three market-structure eras, not the number of bars.

## What this says about our own backtest

Read together, the evidence ranks the likely culprits for a BTC backtest that does not hold up live. The ranking below is inferred from the sources; none of it has yet been measured on our system. **First, selection bias on a short history.** If the strategy's layers, thresholds, stops and lookbacks were tuned over a few years of BTC data and no one logged the number of variants tried, Minimum Backtest Length alone predicts a sizeable in-sample Sharpe from noise. If the market has regime memory, the best-tuned variant should do *worst* live ([Bailey et al. 2014](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)). **Second, the execution model.** Close-price or same-bar fills, a flat fee with maker fills assumed, no funding series and no allowance for fill failure all bias results the same way. On He et al.'s evidence, fees alone can halve a BTC Sharpe ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v5)), and Albers et al. show that slippage then removes more, selectively from the winning trades ([Albers et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v25y2025i6p919-947.html)). **Third, regime-bound parameters.** Fixed price distances calibrated in one volatility regime, or results that depend on two or three crash days, will not carry over to a market whose volatility and kurtosis have moved. **Fourth, the data source.** A single-venue kline feed carries that venue's own wicks, later-revised candles and possibly inflated volume, and the live feed will not match it exactly.

The table turns these causes into tests. It orders them so that the cheapest test which could rule out a cause comes first.

| Live symptom | Most likely cause | Decisive test |
|---|---|---|
| Live Sharpe far below backtest across all regimes | Selection bias / unlogged N | Reconstruct the trial count honestly; compute the Deflated Sharpe (fail below 0.95) and the Harvey-Liu haircut (t > 3); run CSCV/PBO on the full trial P&L matrix (reject above 0.05) |
| Winning trades smaller live, losers unchanged | Adverse-selection fills | Log signal time, intended price and fill price for every live or testnet order; compare against the backtest's assumed fill for the same signal |
| Gap grows with holding period on perps | Missing funding / carry | Re-run the backtest with the venue's historical funding series and taker fees on every market-order and stop leg |
| Performance hinges on a few days or one era | Regime concentration, fat tails | Leave out the top 3 P&L days; split results by volatility regime and market era (pre-2018, 2018-2023, ETF era) |
| Edge sits in wicks, stops or brief extremes | Venue-specific prints, depth collapse | Re-run on a second venue's candles and on the venue's own mark price; apply a one-bar fill delay |
| Volume-based signal or sizing underperforms | Wash-traded or revised volume | Swap reported volume for book-depth proxies, or drop volume features and compare |

Several things remain uncertain. Albers et al.'s slippage magnitudes, the exact median in Suhonen et al., and the Schwenkler vendor-disagreement statistics could not be extracted from the sources. No study reports a backtest-to-live return ratio for a deployed crypto strategy, so the 50-75% decay figures come from traditional markets and are being applied to crypto by analogy. And a testnet is not a full out-of-sample test of execution, because its order book is not the real one.

## Conclusion

The literature points somewhere uncomfortable: a disappointing live result is usually what the backtest should have predicted, not a sign that the market changed. Bailey and colleagues put it this way: "the effect was never there" ([Bailey et al. 2014](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)). In crypto, three of the general failure modes are amplified at once. The history is short, the data carries venue-specific and fabricated artefacts, and execution costs rise exactly when signals fire. So the question for our system is not whether the edge decayed but whether it was ever larger than the noise floor created by our own search, after fee-net, funding-net and adverse-fill costs.

That changes what to work on. Tuning the strategy further cannot fix the gap, because every extra variant raises the bar that the next result has to clear. Progress comes from cutting the trial budget and recording every trial, from validating on several regimes and venues at once instead of a single walk-forward path, and from measuring the backtest-to-live gap order by order before blaming the market. A strategy that survives those tests with a modest deflated Sharpe is worth more than one with a spectacular backtest that no one can account for.
