# BTC Volatility Regimes, Tail Risk and Derivatives Mechanics as Causes of Backtest-to-Live Failure

Scope: this file covers the volatility, regime and derivatives-mechanics half of the crypto-specific analysis. Data quality, market structure and manipulation are covered elsewhere. Research date: 2026-10-30. Source type and approximate date are tagged in brackets on each claim.

## Q1. How extreme are BTC's volatility clustering and fat tails, and how does this break normality / constant-volatility assumptions?

### Takeaway
BTC returns show strong GARCH-type clustering, high persistence and heavy, skewed tails. The tail shape itself also changes by regime: excess kurtosis was about 3.5 in 2017-2019 and about 51 in 2020. A risk model or position-sizing rule calibrated on one window (normal or single-regime GARCH) misstates tail risk in the next one. Regime-switching GARCH clearly beats single-regime GARCH for BTC Value-at-Risk.

### Cited Findings
- A study of daily BTC prices from 2014 to 2019 found that the normal inverse Gaussian (NIG) distribution fit the fat tails and skewness best across GARCH variants, and that the asymmetric tGARCH fit best overall. A normal innovation distribution is therefore misspecified. [Academic preprint, 2019] — [arXiv 1909.04903, "Modelling Bitcoin Returns Volatility using GARCH models"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.04903)
- A high-frequency BTC GARCH study chose GARCH specifically because BTC data shows excess kurtosis and volatility clustering. [Academic preprint, 2018] — [arXiv 1812.09452](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.09452)
- An ARIMA-GARCH(1,1) analysis of 2020-2025 data reports clustering, fat tails and high persistence (alpha + beta of about 0.945), so shocks to volatility decay slowly. [Academic thesis/eprint, ~2025, lower-tier source] — [eprints UMM](https://eprints.umm.ac.id/id/eprint/21904/)
- Contrary result: one comparison of 12 GARCH-type models across seven cryptocurrencies selected IGARCH(1,1) with *normal* innovations by information criteria. The authors note that this departs from the usual fat-tail stylized facts, which shows that model-selection results depend on the sample. [Peer-reviewed, Quantitative Finance and Economics, 2019] — [AIMS QFE 2019](https://aimspress.com/aimspress-data/qfe/2019/4/PDF/QFE-03-04-739.pdf)
- Ardia, Bluteau and Rüede (Finance Research Letters vol. 29, 2019) used Markov-switching GARCH and found strong evidence of regime changes in BTC's GARCH volatility dynamics. MSGARCH beat single-regime GARCH in one-day-ahead VaR forecasts. A single constant-parameter volatility model is therefore misspecified for BTC. [Peer-reviewed, 2019] — [SSRN 3180830](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3180830); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v29y2019icp266-271.html)
- A study covering July 2010 to January 2019 found that BTC conditional volatility has two distinct regimes, high and low. The same literature notes that structural breaks bias GARCH estimates and degrade volatility forecasts (citing Bauwens et al. 2010, 2014). [Academic working paper, Brunel University, ~2018-2019] — [Brunel working paper](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/17274/3/Fulltext.pdf)
- Comparison with equities: a General Tempered Stable fit gave estimated kurtosis of 9.75 for BTC and 8.92 for the S&P 500, both about 3x the normal distribution's value. BTC had 39.73% more propensity than the S&P 500 to produce large daily moves (>1.23% or <-1.06%). [Academic preprint, 2023] — [arXiv 2310.02436, "Bitcoin versus S&P 500 Index: Return and Risk Analysis"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02436)
- Kurtosis is regime-dependent. Over 2017-2019, BTC excess kurtosis (3.46) was *lower* than the S&P 500's (5.41). In 2020 it was 50.87 for BTC versus 8.51 for the S&P 500. [Textbook, Palomar "Portfolio Optimization", ~2024] — [Palomar, sec. 2.3 Non-Gaussianity](https://bookdown.org/palomar/portfoliooptimizationbook/2.3-non-gaussianity-asymmetry-and-heavy-tails.html)
- Dehouche (2021), using daily, weekly and monthly data from 2014 to 2021, found BTC, gold and the S&P 500 all heavy-tailed, with "second moments (variance, standard deviation) non-convergent". For BTC, lower sampling frequency sharply *reduces* kurtosis; the opposite holds for gold and the S&P 500. [Academic preprint, 2021] — [arXiv 2103.00395, "Scale matters"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00395)

### Inferences
- Non-convergent second moments make sample volatility, Sharpe ratio and volatility-target position sizing unstable estimates. The same backtest re-run on a different window can give a materially different risk number, and this instability is a feature of the distribution, not noise to be averaged away.
- The 2017-2019 and 2020 kurtosis numbers show that a strategy whose stops and sizing were tuned in a "thin-tailed" BTC window can meet a fat-tailed window live. Because the tail shape differs between regimes, the problem cannot be fixed by "using fat tails" as a single fixed parameter.
- Kurtosis that depends on frequency matters for strategy design. Intraday and daily strategies face a much fatter-tailed distribution than monthly analyses suggest, so stop-loss and liquidation-distance logic based on lower-frequency statistics will underestimate gap and wick risk.

### Gaps
- I found no single authoritative, up-to-date (2024-2026) table comparing BTC, S&P 500 and gold kurtosis or counts of >4-sigma days on the same window and method. The figures above use different windows and distributions, so they are not directly comparable.
- I did not verify the alpha + beta = 0.945 figure against a peer-reviewed source.

## Q2. How has BTC's market regime changed structurally, and why does that make older data a poor guide?

### Takeaway
BTC's volatility level, participant base and dominant flows have shifted repeatedly. Realized daily volatility fell from about 7.6% in 2012 to about 2.2% in 2025. Two structural breaks stand out: CME/CBOE futures in Dec 2017, after which one study found time-series momentum disappeared, and spot ETFs in Jan 2024, which compressed the basis and reshaped CME open interest. Low realized volatility still coexisted with a record single-day liquidation (Oct 10, 2025). A full-history backtest therefore averages over economically different markets.

### Cited Findings
- K33 Research: BTC realized *daily* volatility was 2.24% for 2025, the lowest annual reading in its series. The series starts in 2012 at 7.58% daily, with 3.34% in 2022 and 2.80% in 2024. Bitwise also noted BTC realized volatility fell below Nvidia's in 2025. [Industry research via news, Dec 2025/Jan 2026] — [KuCoin news flash citing K33](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-volatility-drops-to-2-24-in-2025-lower-than-nvidia); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitcoin-turned-less-volatile-than-nvidia-as-institutional-rails-absorbed-570-billion-in-swings-during-a-boring-year/)
- Glassnode (Feb 21, 2025): BTC 1-week realized volatility fell to 23.42% annualized, near historical lows. It had been lower only a few times in the past four years: Oct 2024 (22.88%) and Nov 2023 (21.35%). [Industry on-chain analytics via news, Feb 2025] — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/bybit-hack-fails-to-disrupt-bitcoins-low-volatility-expectations/)
- Low-volatility regime with tail events: in Oct 2025 BTC fell from about $126,000 to $80,500 (-36%), and the Oct 10 event liquidated about $19B of leveraged positions in a day. [News synthesis, late 2025] — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitcoin-turned-less-volatile-than-nvidia-as-institutional-rails-absorbed-570-billion-in-swings-during-a-boring-year/); [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/forced-liquidations-in-2025-surpass-150-billion/)
- Futures introduction as a regime break: Swinkels (Rotterdam seminar, 2018) studied more than 3,100 cryptocurrencies from Apr 2013 to Aug 2018. He found strong 1-day to 1-month time-series momentum that "seems to have disappeared after the introduction of bitcoin futures" (CME/CBOE, Dec 2017). [Academic seminar abstract, 2018; not verified as peer-reviewed] — [Tinbergen Institute seminar listing](https://utilities.tinbergen.nl/?p=66235)
- ETF-era derivatives structure: a Glassnode figure, quoted secondhand, puts CME BTC futures open interest at more than 212,000 BTC at the start of 2025 and about 130,000 BTC later that year, roughly the level at the Jan 2024 spot-ETF launch. Velo data put the annualized basis below 10% all year in 2025, versus about 20% at end-2024. [News citing Glassnode/Velo, late 2025; syndicated, not checked against primary] — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/what-causes-bitcoin-price-to-move-up-or-down-at-2-1t-market-cap-in-2025/)
- 2022 regime (Terra/Luna, Celsius/3AC, FTX): BTC fell about 65% in calendar 2022 (The Block). Other accounts cite about 75%, which is likely peak-to-trough from the Nov 2021 high (the two sources measure differently). Chainalysis estimated realized investor losses of $20.5B from Terra/Luna, $33B from Celsius/3AC and about $9B from FTX. Nansen and an academic on-chain study identify the Terra collapse as the trigger of the liquidity crunch that later broke FTX/Alameda. [News citing industry reports, Dec 2022-Sep 2023] — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/197109/bitcoin-plunged-65-in-2022-as-terra-ftx-went-up-in-flames); [Forkast citing Chainalysis](https://forkast.news/headlines/ftx-collapse-not-the-worst-for-crypto-investors-this-year-says-chainalysis-study/); [Bitcoin.com citing Nansen](https://news.bitcoin.com/analysis-of-ftx-and-alameda-collapse-points-to-terra-luna-fallout-starting-the-domino-effect/); [UJI repository](https://repositori.uji.es/xmlui/handle/10234/204236?show=full)
- Basis/carry regime: Schmeling, Schrimpf and Todorov (BIS WP 1087, Apr 2023) report that crypto futures carry averaged above 10% p.a. and reached up to 60% p.a., far above carry in equities, rates, FX or commodities. Retail demand for leveraged upside in booms drove it. [Central-bank working paper, 2023] — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)
- He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter: deviations of crypto perpetual prices from no-arbitrage values are larger than in traditional FX markets, co-move across coins and "shrink over time" as markets mature. [Academic preprint, Dec 2022, revised Aug 2024] — [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888v6)

### Inferences
- A 2013-2025 backtest mixes a market with about 7.6% daily volatility (2012) and one with about 2.2% (2025), a roughly 3.4x ratio. Fixed-percentage stops, fixed take-profits, breakout thresholds and fixed-notional sizing will all behave very differently across that history. Strategies with fixed price-distance parameters are most exposed.
- Edges tied to market immaturity tend to decay: large perpetual mispricings, 10-60% carry, and pre-futures time-series momentum. The decay coincides with institutional entry (CME 2017, ETFs 2024). Early-sample returns overstate what is available now.
- Low average volatility does not mean low tail risk. The 2025 record-low annual volatility sits alongside a record liquidation day, so volatility-scaled sizing can increase leverage just before a cascade.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a primary source with quantitative metrics for Mt. Gox (2014) or the 2017 ICO-mania regime (volatility levels, liquidity, participant mix). The 2012 K33 volatility figure is the only pre-2017 quantitative anchor here.
- I did not find a peer-reviewed formal structural-break test (e.g. Bai-Perron) dating BTC regimes at the CME (2017) and ETF (2024) boundaries. That timing is inferred from the sources above, not tested directly in them.
- The CME open interest and basis figures come from syndicated news citing Glassnode/Velo; I could not confirm them against primary dashboards.

## Q3. How do perpetual funding dynamics and liquidation cascades distort price action in ways a spot-only backtest misses?

### Takeaway
Perpetual funding, carry and forced deleveraging produce price paths that are endogenous to positioning: cascades through liquidation clusters, basis compression that forces "hedged" carry trades to unwind, ADL, and order-book depth collapses of more than 90%. A spot-price backtest sees the price outcome but not the mechanism. It cannot model the fills, slippage, funding costs or ADL a live leveraged strategy would actually face, and it does not know where the fragile price levels were.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanism: perpetuals do not expire and are not guaranteed to converge to spot. Longs periodically pay shorts a funding rate tied to the futures-spot gap, which keeps the perpetual near spot. Standard no-arbitrage pricing does not apply directly. [Academic preprint, 2022-2024] — [He, Manela, Ross, von Wachter, arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888v6)
- High carry predicts crashes: BIS finds "a high crypto carry predicts future price crashes," with rising carry accompanied by higher prices for crash insurance. Arbitrageurs running cash-and-carry face "spikes in margins and liquidations amid drawdowns". Together with high leverage, the authors argue this helps explain why severe crashes are frequent in crypto. [BIS WP 1087, Apr 2023] — [BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)
- Christin, Routledge, Soska and Zetlin-Jones ("The Crypto Carry Trade", Aug 2022) found the carry trade (short futures, long spot) unusually profitable, with *in-sample* annual Sharpe ratios of about 7-10. [Academic working paper, 2022] — [CMU PDF](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/azj/files/CarryTrade.v1.0.pdf)
- 2025 carry decay and unwind: the 30-day annualized BTC basis peaked at 15.3% in Jan 2025, more than 10 points above T-bills. At writing the readings were 7-day 8.1%, 30-day 5.4% and 90-day 4.6%. Assuming a 4.5% T-bill rate, excess carry was positive on only 177 of 365 days and above 5% on 31 days (8%). The three "unattractive" regimes covered 246 days (67% of the year). In Oct 2025, falling spot compressed the basis, "hedged" carry positions lost money, and risk limits forced unwinds (sell spot, buy futures) that added to downside pressure. Some ETF outflows reflected arbitrage unwinds rather than fundamental selling. [Industry research, Amberdata/Kaiko, July 2025 with Oct data; the page has internal arithmetic inconsistencies, e.g. 31 vs 23 attractive days] — [Kaiko/Amberdata](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/the-carry-trade-that-broke-how-basis-compression-triggered-the-cascade)
- CME basis compression: in Feb 2025 the CME basis traded below 10% annualized for most of the month, with a low of 4.08% on Feb 24, during sustained ETF outflows. [Industry research, Galaxy, Mar 2025] — [Galaxy Research](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/perspectives/bitcoin-sell-off-drives-basis-compression-etf-outflows-and-risk-off)
- Liquidation clusters: Glassnode (Sep 23, 2025) argues that when "many traders share similar liquidation thresholds" those levels become fragile, and price entering them accelerates unwinds and volatility. Hyperliquid liquidation bands "tend to align closely" with spikes in aggregate liquidations on Binance, BitMEX, Bybit and OKX, and larger concentrations line up with larger spikes. Hyperliquid was about 16% of global open interest. The article does *not* claim a "magnet" effect or deliberate stop-hunting. [Industry research, Sep 2025] — [Glassnode Research](https://research.glassnode.com/liquidation-heatmaps/)
- Oct 10, 2025 cascade: more than $19B of liquidations in about a day, an all-time record. ForkLog suggests the true figure may have been $30-40B once reporting lags and market-maker data are included (an estimate, not verified). Hyperliquid handled $10.08B, Bybit $4.58B and Binance $2.31B. Top-of-book BTC depth fell by more than 90% on key venues as market makers widened spreads or withdrew. The trigger was the US 100% China tariff announcement, amplified by 24/7 trading with no circuit breakers. [News/consultancy, Oct-Nov 2025] — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/forced-liquidations-in-2025-surpass-150-billion/); [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/ja-jp/japan/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity); [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)
- ADL: when liquidations and insurance or backstop vaults are exhausted, exchanges force-close *profitable* positions on the winning side. Hyperliquid said its waterfall went order book, then liquidity vaults, then ADL. Exchanges dispute the causes: OKX's CEO blamed Binance marketing campaigns, and Crypto.com's CEO called for an investigation. [News, Oct 2025] — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained); [Cointelegraph via TradingView](https://th.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:83d396b6a094b:0-19b-crypto-market-crash-was-it-leverage-china-tariffs-or-both)
- Microstructure during the cascade: a minute-level panel of Hyperliquid BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP/DOGE perpetual books showed the pooled median quoted spread reached 9.4 bps at 21:00 UTC on Oct 10, with p95 at 87.5 bps, and recovered within the day. [Journal article by an independent researcher, 2026; peer-review status unverified] — [JFDA, Lincoln](https://journals.lincoln.ac.nz/index.php/JFDA/article/view/1395)
- Exchange-driven forced closures, rather than panic selling alone, drove much of the Oct 2025 losses. [News, Oct 2025] — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/344141/explaining-bitcoin-crash-liquidations-leverage)

### Inferences
- A spot OHLC backtest records the cascade wick but assumes fills that were not available: depth fell more than 90% and p95 spreads were about 87 bps on one venue. A stop or limit sitting inside a liquidation cluster fills far worse live, or not at all.
- A leveraged perpetual strategy has P&L paths a spot backtest omits entirely: funding payments, which can flip sign and are largest in crowded booms; ADL of *winning* positions, so a profitable hedge can be force-closed; and exchange margin changes.
- Carry and basis strategies are short tail risk. Their high in-sample Sharpe ratios (about 7-10 in-sample, CMU 2022) come from long calm stretches with the crash risk paid out rarely (BIS: high carry predicts crashes). Their mark-to-market losses come exactly when liquidity vanishes, so a backtest with too few crash episodes overstates them.
- Price levels are partly endogenous to open-interest positioning. Spot-only data has no open-interest or liquidation-level features, so any signal behaving differently near those levels (breakouts, stops, mean-reversion entries) is untestable on spot data alone.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that quantifies deliberate stop-hunting or a "magnet" pull toward liquidation clusters. Glassnode explicitly makes only a correlational claim, and stop-hunting claims remain practitioner folklore in the sources found.
- I found no primary-source time series of BTC perpetual funding rates across cycles (e.g. 2021 peaks versus the ETF era) in this pass.
- The quantitative strength of BIS's "high carry predicts crashes" result (coefficients, hit rate) was not on the abstract page.

## Q4. Does BTC's short history (few independent regimes or cycles) inflate false confidence?

### Takeaway
Yes. BTC has only three complete halving cycles, about four boom-bust episodes, and every cycle occurred under different macro and market-structure conditions. Commentators and even pro-cycle researchers concede that such samples cannot establish statistical regularities. Even calendar-rich daily data contains few *independent* tail and regime events.

### Cited Findings
- BTC has only three complete halvings (2012, 2016, 2020) plus a handful of traded years, "too little to establish a law". On such thin samples almost any pattern eventually surfaces, and survivorship bias keeps the regularities that happened to work. [Commentary, ~2025-2026] — [eco3min](https://eco3min.fr/en/crypto-seasonality-cycles-signal-illusion/); [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/bitcoin-cycle-predictions-lack-statistical-foundation)
- Each cycle unfolded in a different macro setting (rates, liquidity, the 2024 arrival of spot ETFs). The halving supply shock is mechanically smaller each time, and halving-cycle returns have shrunk every cycle. Global liquidity is an equally plausible explanation of the apparent cycle. [Industry news, ~2024] — [Blockworks](https://blockworks-editorial-9odybgvzo-blockworks.vercel.app/news/bitcoin-halving-cycle-returns)
- Even a pro-cycle paper ("Bitcoin Runs on a Clock", 2026) concedes its evidence is "descriptive and economic rather than statistically significant per epoch". Its out-of-sample window is one 526-day span dominated by a single bear market: "suggestive, not decisive". [Academic preprint, 2026; also Quantpedia summary] — [arXiv 2607.26188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26188); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/bitcoin-runs-on-a-clock-why-every-price-indicator-dies-and-the-halving-clock-doesnt/?a=6080)
- Heavy tails make the problem worse: with non-convergent variance (Dehouche 2021), sample moments from short histories are unreliable. [Academic preprint, 2021] — [arXiv 2103.00395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00395)
- Crypto momentum results depend on the sample. Weekly data from 2013-2019 gave insignificant negative returns, a 2015-2018 sample showed large payoffs, and 2014-2018 monthly data showed no support. The literature has no consensus. [Academic review in a peer-reviewed journal, 2025] — [Springer, Financial Markets and Portfolio Management, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)

### Inferences
- The effective sample size for regime-dependent edges is the number of regimes, about 3-4 BTC cycles and 1-2 market-structure eras (pre-futures, futures, ETF), not the number of daily bars. A Sharpe ratio estimated from about 4 cycles has a confidence interval wide enough to include zero for most plausible edges.
- Because each cycle differs structurally (volatility level, leverage venue, participant base), cycles are not even i.i.d. draws. Walk-forward results from one era are weak evidence for the next era.
- Combined with heavy tails, the few catastrophic events in the sample (Mar 2020, May 2021, Terra/FTX 2022, Oct 2025) dominate realized P&L for many strategies. A backtest's result can hinge on whether two or three days are included.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that formally computes an "effective number of independent observations" or a Deflated Sharpe Ratio specifically for BTC strategies. The López de Prado / Bailey multiple-testing literature is general, not BTC-specific, and was not retrieved in this pass.

## Q5. Documented cases of crypto strategies that worked in one era and failed in a later one

### Takeaway
The best-documented cases are: (1) time-series momentum, strong in 2013-2017 but reported to have disappeared after the Dec 2017 BTC futures launch; (2) the carry / basis / perpetual-arbitrage trade, with in-sample Sharpe ratios of about 7-10 and carry of 10-60% in earlier years, shrinking deviations as markets matured, and only marginal excess returns over T-bills for most of 2025 plus a forced unwind in Oct 2025; and (3) crypto momentum's exposure to sudden crashes. Strategies that worked in one era of these markets often failed in the next.

### Cited Findings
- Time-series momentum on more than 3,100 coins (Apr 2013 to Aug 2018) was strong at 1-day to 1-month horizons and "seems to have disappeared after the introduction of bitcoin futures". Cross-sectional momentum at 1-day to 2-week horizons remained. [Seminar abstract, Swinkels, 2018; not verified as published] — [Tinbergen Institute](https://utilities.tinbergen.nl/?p=66235)
- Crypto momentum is subject to severe crashes, and a single coin can render momentum portfolio returns insignificant. Volatility management is suggested as a mitigation. [Academic thesis, Univ. of Vaasa, "Cryptocurrency momentum has (not) its moments"] — [Osuva/UWasa](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/handle/10024/20018)
- Counter-evidence on persistence: a 2020-2025 study of eight major coins reports that time-series momentum remains economically meaningful. It does not compare pre- and post-futures periods directly. [Academic journal, Business: Theory and Practice, ~2025] — [VU journals](https://www.journals.vu.lt/BATP/en/article/view/44540)
- Carry decay: in-sample annual Sharpe of about 7-10 (CMU, 2022 data); perpetual mispricings shrink as markets mature (He et al.); 2025 30-day basis mostly 3.6-6.4% in non-euphoric regimes, against a T-bill rate of about 4.5%, with excess returns of Q2 -1.0% and Q3 0.0%; an October unwind amplified the selling. [Academic 2022-2024; industry 2025] — [CMU](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/azj/files/CarryTrade.v1.0.pdf); [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888v6); [Kaiko/Amberdata](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/the-carry-trade-that-broke-how-basis-compression-triggered-the-cascade)
- Retail-funded carry: a CryptoSlate carry example uses a 6.3% basis and a 3.8% bill rate, about 2.5% before frictions. If a desk funds with an exchange stablecoin and borrows at 3-6%, the spread can fall to about zero or go negative after fees. [News analysis, 2025] — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/what-causes-bitcoin-price-to-move-up-or-down-at-2-1t-market-cap-in-2025/)
- Indicator decay generally: the 2026 "Bitcoin Runs on a Clock" preprint is titled around the observation that "every price indicator dies", meaning price-based cycle indicators that worked in past cycles stopped working. [Preprint, 2026; the authors' own framing] — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/bitcoin-runs-on-a-clock-why-every-price-indicator-dies-and-the-halving-clock-doesnt/?a=6080)
- Recent out-of-sample claims should be read with care: a 2026 preprint reports a trend-following Sharpe of 2.41 out-of-sample over 2022-2024. Its methodology was unverified, and it covers a single era. [Preprint, 2026] — [arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)

### Inferences
- The pattern across cases is consistent: edges that depended on market immaturity (retail leverage demand, slow arbitrage capital, no regulated futures, no ETF arbitrage) decayed as institutional capital arrived. A backtest dominated by 2013-2021 data overweights a market that no longer exists.
- Mean-reversion strategies are the mirror image of the momentum and carry cases. They tend to look strong in low-volatility, range-bound regimes (e.g. 2023-2025, record-low realized volatility) and fail in liquidation cascades. This is an inference from the volatility and cascade evidence above, not from a documented case study.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous, documented case study of a specific BTC *mean-reversion* strategy failing across eras. This needs a targeted search (e.g. Quantpedia or SSRN "bitcoin mean reversion out-of-sample").
- I did not retrieve the full text of the Springer 2025 momentum review (access-redirected), so its own findings beyond the cited literature summary are unconfirmed.
- I did not find a published replication of the Swinkels post-futures momentum disappearance.
