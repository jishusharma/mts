# Crypto/BTC Data Quality and Market Structure: Why Backtests Fail or Mislead

Source-type legend used below: [PEER] peer-reviewed journal; [WP] working paper / preprint; [REG] regulator or court filing / DOJ; [IND] industry/vendor research; [PRESS] journalism (corroboration only); [ANEC] anecdotal / self-interested claim.

## 1. How reliable is historical crypto OHLCV / order-book data? (outages, gaps, vendor disagreement)

### Takeaway
There is no "golden source" for crypto market data: a 2025 Management Science audit found major vendors disagree materially on prices, volumes and market caps and mislabel assets, and single venues routinely print prices (flash crashes, outages, venue-internal depegs) that never existed elsewhere. A backtest's results therefore depend on which vendor and which venue was chosen, often more than on the strategy.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER, 2025] Schwenkler, Shah and Yang, "Aggregate Confusion in Crypto Market Data" (Management Science, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2025.00611) audits leading crypto data vendors and reports widespread mislabeling, unstable asset identifiers, and large discrepancies between providers in prices, market caps and volumes; proposes an aggregation method that filters unreliable observations plus a data-quality index and vendor grading. Covers eight providers (incl. CoinGecko), with an appendix surveying about 20 vendors (incl. CoinMarketCap). Authors argue the inconsistencies can distort empirical research and investment analysis — [INFORMS abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00611); [Supplement](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00611/suppl_file/mnsc.2025.00611.sm1.pdf); version presented to SEC (Oct 2025) — [SEC file](https://www.sec.gov/file/cryptodata-sec-102525)
- [PRESS, 2019] CoinMarketCap itself acknowledged that concerns over inaccurate data on its site were "valid" (Bloomberg, Mar 2019) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-03-25/crypto-aggregator-says-concerns-over-inaccurate-data-are-valid)
- [IND] Candle data is revised after the fact: some trades are not collected in real time, are backfilled with delay, and candles are later recalculated (Talos/Coin Metrics candle-data note). Implication: a candle downloaded today can differ from the one a live system saw at the time — [Talos](https://www.talos.com/insights/improvements-to-coin-metrics-candles-data)
- [IND] Vendors run their own cross-exchange comparison and outlier filtering (e.g., CoinAPI's multi-layer quality controls), meaning "cleaned" vendor series embed vendor-specific judgements — [CoinAPI docs](https://docs.coinapi.io/indexes-api/data-inputs-and-sources/data-quality-controls)
- [IND, vendor claim] Coin Metrics markets its reference price as robust to "outliers, market manipulation and artifacts in the market microstructure" — vendor claim, not independent evidence — [search summary of Coin Metrics material](https://mwm.ai/apps/coin-metrics-crypto-data/1524880601)
- [PRESS, Oct 21 2021] Venue-specific flash crash: BTC on Binance.US fell from ~$65,760 to $8,200 (~87%) and recovered within one minute at 11:34 UTC; Binance.US blamed a bug in an institutional client's algorithm. On other major exchanges BTC only dipped ~$65,500 -> $64,200; Coinbase did not crash because of price-protection points — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/121657/binance-us-blames-bitcoin-flash-crash-to-8200-on-a-bug-in-a-clients-trading-algorithm); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tr/markets/2021/10/21/bitcoin-price-flash-crash-on-binanceus-attributed-to-trader-algorithm-bug)
- [PRESS, Mar 13 2020] Exchange outage at the worst moment: BitMEX (then the largest BTC perp venue) went offline/delayed during the "Black Thursday" crash; it first cited a cloud-provider hardware issue (02:16-02:40 UTC, resumed 03:00), later attributed it to a DDoS attack. BTC hit ~$3,700 then rebounded to ~$5,500; BitMEX had more liquidations that day than any 24h in the prior year. Accounts of cause/timing conflict — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitmex-denies-it-made-bitcoin-price-drop-to-37k-after-going-offline); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/exchange/bitmex-caught-in-cross-hairs-of-two-attacks-in-one-month-says-cto/)
- [WP/IND, Oct 10 2025] Venue-internal depeg: USDe traded down to ~$0.65 on Binance while holding near $1 on all other venues and on-chain; Binance's oracle was derived primarily from its own order book, and the venue had deposit/withdrawal issues. Duration ~90 min per Dune; compensation figures conflict ($283M Yahoo vs >$328M preprint) — [21Shares research](https://www.21shares.com//research/why-did-ethenas-stablecoin-remain-stable-onchain-but-depegged-on-binance); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/usde-depeg-oracle-issues-ethena-founder?rand=26097); [Dune Digest](https://dune.com/blog/32); [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9459584/v1)

### Inferences
- A backtest using a single-venue candle feed (e.g., Binance klines via CCXT) will contain venue-specific wicks (Binance.US $8,200 style prints) that trigger stops/entries in simulation; whether that venue's wick "really happened" for a strategy depends on whether it would have been filled there, with what size and latency.
- Outages cluster in high-volatility moments (BitMEX 2020, Binance 2025). Backtests assume the venue was reachable; live systems face rejected/delayed orders exactly when the strategy's edge (or stop) mattered most. Gaps in candles during outages are often silently forward-filled or interpolated by vendors.
- Because vendors backfill and re-filter, results from a vendor-cleaned series can be irreproducible against the raw data a live bot actually streamed; point-in-time (as-first-seen) data is the honest benchmark.
- Any strategy keyed on a venue's mark/index/oracle price (liquidation-based or basis trades) must use that venue's own index history, not a cross-venue composite, and vice versa.

### Gaps
- The quantitative magnitudes from Schwenkler/Shah/Yang (percent of observations disagreeing, typical price/volume gap sizes) could not be extracted: the INFORMS page returned 403 and the SEC PDF could not be parsed locally. The report writer should cite the qualitative findings only.
- No independent, published head-to-head benchmark of Kaiko vs Coin Metrics vs CryptoCompare vs CCXT-pulled candles for identical BTC candles was found.
- No systematic, sourced catalog of Binance/Coinbase kline gaps or API-outage minutes was found; the outage evidence above is incident-level (press).
- Order-book (L2/L3) historical data quality (snapshot vs diff reconstruction, sequence-number gaps) was not covered by any citable study found.

## 2. Wash trading / fake volume: quantified studies and implications for backtesting on reported volume/liquidity

### Takeaway
Multiple independent lines of evidence (Bitwise 2019 to SEC, peer-reviewed Cong et al. 2023 and Aloosh & Li 2024, Forbes 2022, regulator complaints and a 2024 FBI sting) show a large share of reported crypto volume is fabricated, concentrated on unregulated venues and small tokens; BTC on top regulated venues is far cleaner. Any backtest using exchange-reported volume, turnover, or "liquidity" (for signals, position sizing, slippage models or universe filters) is at risk of being built on fake numbers.

### Cited Findings
- [IND/ANEC-ish, Mar 2019; submitted to SEC] Bitwise's presentation to the SEC (226 slides, supporting its ETF filing) concluded ~95% of reported BTC volume was fake or non-economic: of 81 exchanges reporting >$1M BTC volume on CoinMarketCap, only 10 had real volume, accounting for ~5% of reported volume; real daily volume ~$273M vs ~$6B reported. Method: trade-size histograms, volume-spike alignment, spread patterns. Motive suggested: exchange listing fees ($1-3M). Note Bitwise had a commercial interest (ETF approval) — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitwise-calls-out-to-sec-95-of-bitcoin-trade-volume-is-fake-real-market-is-or); [CrowdFundInsider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2019/05/147183-bitwise-presentation-to-sec-provides-insight-into-btc-etf-push-as-well-as-bogus-crypto-exchange-marketplace/)
- [PEER, 2023] Cong, Li, Tang, Yang, "Crypto Wash Trading" (Management Science 69(11):6427-6454; NBER w30783): statistical tests (first-significant-digit/Benford, trade-size rounding/clustering, tail distributions) on 29 centralized exchanges. Regulated exchanges show patterns like traditional markets; unregulated exchanges show wash trading averaging over 70% of reported volume. Fabricated volume improves exchange rankings and temporarily distorts prices — [NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w30783); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:69:y:2023:i:11:p:6427-6454); [Reading repository](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/113166)
- [PEER, 2024] Aloosh & Li, "Direct Evidence of Bitcoin Wash Trading" (Management Science): using leaked Mt. Gox trade-level data with trader IDs on both sides, identify >115,000 wash trades by 2,887 trader IDs (~2.1% of transactions, ~1.4% of volume during the active period), involving known exchange insiders; consistent with (not proven) exchange-driven volume inflation. Validates Benford's law, trade-size clustering, lognormality and structural-break tests as useful detectors; power-law tail measures give opposite conclusions — [DCU working paper PDF](https://doras.dcu.ie/32898/1/Direct%20evidence%20of%20bitcoin%20wash%20trading.pdf); [GMU news](https://business.gmu.edu/news/2024-08/self-dealing-has-long-history-crypto-exchanges)
- [PEER, 2021] Le Pennec, Fiedler, Ante, "Wash trading at cryptocurrency exchanges" (Finance Research Letters 43): using wallet balances/counts and web traffic rather than trade statistics, finds 96-98% of volume on the most suspicious exchanges highly questionable — [EconBiz record](https://econbiz.de/Record/wash-trading-at-cryptocurrency-exchanges-pennec-gu%C3%A9nol%C3%A9/10014632436); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/detecting-wash-trading-in-major-crypto-exchanges/)
- [PRESS/IND, Aug 2022] Forbes analysis of 157 exchanges estimated real global BTC daily volume on June 14, 2022 at ~$128B vs ~$262B self-reported (51% overstatement); least-regulated exchanges claimed ~$217B vs ~$89B estimated. Forbes itself cautioned there is no universally accepted method — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/more-than-50-of-reported-bitcoin-trading-volume-is-likely-to-be-fake-or-non-economic-report); [Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2022/sep/20/half-all-bitcoin-transactions-are-fake-new-study-f/); [Forklog breakdown: 48 venues 0-25%, 73 venues 26-79%, 36 venues 80-99% discrepancy](https://forklog.com/en/news/forbes-more-than-half-of-bitcoin-trades-on-exchanges-are-fake)
- [IND] Coin Metrics "Trusted Volume" filters exchanges by volume correlation with benchmark exchanges, web-traffic ratios and qualitative checks (spot only); ~13 exchanges passed in 2019 and real volumes were ~1/10 of reported per Decrypt's coverage; ~$40B weekly-average trusted volume as of April 2024, described as a lower bound — [Coin Metrics docs](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/asset-metrics/volume/volume_trusted_spot_usd_1d); [Coin Metrics TEF 2.0](https://coinmetrics.io/special-insights/trusted-exchange-framework-2-0/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/37027/crypto-exchanges-market-trading-volume-coin-metrics); [CrowdFundInsider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2024/05/224657-auditability-of-data-in-crypto-is-one-of-the-blockchain-industrys-most-defining-characteristics-report/)
- [REG, Mar 2023] CFTC complaint alleged Binance traded on its own platform via ~300 undisclosed "house accounts" owned directly or indirectly by Changpeng Zhao, exempt from its insider-trading policy (this is self-trading, not explicitly a wash-trading allegation). Binance denied manipulation — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/223143/binance-details-from-cftc-complaint)
- [REG, Jun 2023] SEC complaint alleged Binance.US volume was inflated via wash trading through dozens of accounts held by Sigma Chain, a Zhao-controlled firm; Binance called this "entirely unfounded" — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/sec-binance-us-wash-traded-ceo-dismiss-ctfc-complaint)
- [REG, Oct 2024] DOJ "Operation Token Mirrors": FBI created its own token (NexFundAI) to catch market makers; ZM Quant, CLS Global, MyTrade charged with wash trading, Gotbit charged for a similar scheme; 18 people/entities charged, >$25M seized, bots shut down that manipulated ~60 tokens ("market-manipulation-as-a-service") — [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/eighteen-individuals-and-entities-charged-international-operation-targeting-widespread); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/285503/feds-charge-gotbit-others-market-manipulation)
- [PEER, 2018] Gandal, Hamrick, Moore, Oberman, "Price Manipulation in the Bitcoin Ecosystem" (Journal of Monetary Economics): ~600,000 BTC (~$188M) fraudulently acquired on Mt. Gox by bots "Markus" and "Willy"; Willy bought ~268,132 BTC for ~$112M without depositing fiat; USD-BTC rate rose ~4% on suspicious-trade days; fake trades inflated Mt. Gox's reported volume. Claimed link to 2013 rise from $150 to $1,000 — [authors' WEIS 2017 slides](https://tylermoore.utulsa.edu/weis17pres.pdf); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/15/researchers-finds-that-one-person-likely-drove-bitcoin-from-150-to-1000)
- [IND, 2025] Chainalysis estimated up to $2.57B suspected wash-trading volume in ERC-20/BEP-20 tokens on select chains (Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base), concentrated among few actors; overlapping heuristics mean totals are not exact. A separate Chainalysis 2024 figure (~$704M / ~$1.87B by two methods) conflicts and was not reconciled — [Cryptonews](https://cryptonews.com/news/chainalysis-market-manipulation-billions-trades/); [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/content/43347725/chainalysis-report-market-manipulation-impacts-billions-in-crypto-trading-volume)

### Inferences
- Estimates of the fake share span ~1.4% (Mt. Gox, direct evidence, 2011-14) to ~95% (Bitwise 2019, all CMC-listed venues); the spread is mostly explained by venue mix: regulated/top venues look roughly normal, unregulated long-tail venues are mostly fake. For BTC on Binance/Coinbase/Kraken in recent years, volume is far more trustworthy than 2017-2019 aggregate figures, but not certified clean (CFTC/SEC allegations against Binance/Binance.US).
- Volume-based features (volume breakouts, OBV, VWAP anchors, "volume confirms trend", volume-weighted universe ranking, turnover factors) trained on pre-2020 or aggregator data are especially suspect; the "signal" may partly be exchange marketing activity.
- Slippage/capacity models that scale with reported volume will understate costs where volume is inflated: displayed volume overstates the depth a real order can access.
- Wash-trading prevalence changes over time (regulation, ranking methodology changes at CMC, enforcement), so a volume feature's statistical relationship is non-stationary for structural, not market, reasons.

### Gaps
- Primary Bitwise SEC deck (sec.gov comment file) was not fetched; figures are from consistent secondary coverage.
- No peer-reviewed estimate of wash-trading share specifically for BTC spot on Binance/Coinbase in 2023-2026 was found.
- No source quantified wash/self-trading in BTC perpetual futures volume (where most BTC volume now trades).

## 3. Crypto-specific survivorship bias (delisted coins, defunct exchanges)

### Takeaway
Survivorship bias in crypto is large and documented: one study of 3,904 coins (2014-2021) estimates ~62% per year bias for equal-weighted portfolios (0.93% value-weighted) and finds the size premium overstated by ~50% in survivor-only samples. Defunct venues (Mt. Gox, FTX, BitMEX-era dominance) also drop out of "today's" datasets, which erases exactly the history where counterparty failure occurred.

### Cited Findings
- [WP 2022 / PEER per repository] Ammann, Burdorf, Liebi, Stöckl, "Survivorship and Delisting Bias in Cryptocurrency Markets": 3,904 cryptocurrencies, 2014-2021; annualized bias 0.93% (value-weighted) and 62.19% (equal-weighted); size effect confirmed but premium overestimated by 50% in a survival-conditioned sample; no robust positive relation between returns and one-week momentum, market beta, or downside risk; selection bias explains much of the variation in prior studies' results — [working paper page](https://diffform.netlify.app/publication/2022_liebi_survivorship); [University of St. Gallen repository](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/items/bd8eb8d2-428c-47d2-b8a6-cc319f252037). Note: repository listing places it in Financial Markets and Portfolio Management; published vs working-paper wording differs slightly.
- [PEER, 2024] The only trade-level evidence of BTC wash trading comes from a dead exchange's leaked data (Mt. Gox, collapsed 2014), i.e., the defunct venue's data exists only because it was hacked — [DCU working paper](https://doras.dcu.ie/32898/1/Direct%20evidence%20of%20bitcoin%20wash%20trading.pdf)
- [PEER, 2018] Mt. Gox was the dominant BTC venue in 2013 and its price was manipulated by the Willy/Markus bots; histories that splice in Mt. Gox data import manipulated prints, while histories that start from surviving venues omit that era — [WEIS slides](https://tylermoore.utulsa.edu/weis17pres.pdf)
- [IND, 2025] Chainalysis: of >3M tokens created in 2024, ~1.29M (42.5%) listed on a DEX, and only 1.7% were actively traded in the last month — evidence of very high token "mortality" — [Cryptorank summary of Chainalysis](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/6a65e-89-of-dex-pools-exploited-by-creators-in-pump-and-dump-schemes-chainalysis); [Cryptonews](https://cryptonews.com/news/chainalysis-market-manipulation-billions-trades/)

### Inferences
- For a BTC-only strategy the coin-level survivorship problem is small (BTC survived), but venue-level survivorship is real: data from FTX (collapsed Nov 2022), Mt. Gox, BTC-e etc. is absent from current exchange APIs, so a backtest using "Binance since 2017" never experiences a counterparty-failure event, and would not model funds locked on a failing venue.
- Pulling the current exchange's listed pairs (e.g., via CCXT `load_markets`) and backfilling is survivor-conditioned by construction: delisted pairs vanish from the API; cross-sectional crypto strategies (momentum, size, low-cap rotations) built this way inflate returns, consistent with the ~62%/yr EW bias estimate.
- Delisting returns are typically near -100% (or untradeable) and are not in candle data; a backtest must explicitly impute them.

### Gaps
- The study's handling of delisting returns (imputed value) was not retrievable from the abstract page.
- No study found that quantifies venue-level survivorship bias (e.g., performance difference when including vs excluding FTX/Mt. Gox histories) for BTC strategies.
- No sourced figure for the number of exchanges that have closed since 2010 was found in this pass.

## 4. Fragmentation: no consolidated tape — what is "the price"?

### Takeaway
Crypto trades on dozens of venues with no consolidated tape or NBBO; peer-reviewed work shows large, persistent cross-venue and cross-country price gaps (especially pre-2018), and recent events show single venues printing prices far from the rest of the market. "BTC price" in a backtest is a modelling choice (one venue, a composite index, or a mark price), and fills are only realistic on the venue whose prices were used.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER, 2020] Makarov & Schoar, "Trading and arbitrage in cryptocurrency markets" (Journal of Financial Economics 135(2):293-319): large, recurrent arbitrage opportunities across exchanges that often persist for weeks; gaps much larger across countries than within, pointing to capital controls limiting arbitrage capital; cross-country gaps co-move and widen when BTC rises sharply; a common component explains 80% of bitcoin returns — [RePEc abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:2:p:293-319)
- [PRESS, secondary] Korea "kimchi premium": BTC price in Korea can rise up to ~20% above US prices (secondary figure; verify in paper) — [Decrypt "Professor Coin"](https://decrypt.co/245963/professor-coin-free-lunch-cryptocurrency-markets?amp=1)
- [WP 2023, via PRESS] Crépellière, Pelster & Zeisberger follow-up (as summarized by Decrypt) found cross-exchange arbitrage profits fell considerably after April 2018 and are no longer exploitable by investors after costs — [Decrypt (Spanish)](https://decrypt.co/es/246716/profesor-cripto-existe-un-almuerzo-gratis-en-los-mercados-de-criptomonedas)
- [PRESS, 2021] Single-venue print divergence: Binance.US $8,200 vs ~$64,200 elsewhere in the same minute; Kraken reportedly dipped to $54,000 momentarily — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/121657/binance-us-blames-bitcoin-flash-crash-to-8200-on-a-bug-in-a-clients-trading-algorithm); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/news/binance-blames-trading-algorithm-bug-for-bitcoin-flash-crash/)
- [IND, 2025] USDe at ~$0.65 on Binance vs ~$1 elsewhere; Binance's oracle referenced its own order book rather than the deepest liquidity — shows venue-defined prices drove liquidations that a composite-price backtest would never show — [21Shares](https://www.21shares.com//research/why-did-ethenas-stablecoin-remain-stable-onchain-but-depegged-on-binance)
- [PEER, 2020] Griffin & Shams, "Is Bitcoin Really Untethered?" (Journal of Finance 75(4):1913-1964): Tether flows from one large account on Bitfinex timed after market downturns are associated with large subsequent BTC price increases during the 2017 boom, consistent with price support/manipulation rather than investor demand. Bitfinex/Tether denied manipulation — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:75:y:2020:i:4:p:1913-1964); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3195066)

### Inferences
- Quote-currency fragmentation matters: BTC/USDT, BTC/USD, BTC/USDC and BTC/KRW are different instruments; USDT-quoted series embed stablecoin peg risk and (per Griffin & Shams) possibly stablecoin-issuance-driven price paths in 2017.
- Strategies whose backtested edge comes from wicks, intraday extremes or brief dislocations are most venue-sensitive; strategies on daily closes are less so, but "close" itself differs by venue and by the vendor's day boundary (UTC vs other).
- Spot vs perpetual vs futures prices differ by funding/basis; backtesting a perp strategy on spot candles (or vice versa) misstates both entries and liquidation triggers, which use the venue's mark/index price.
- Historical arbitrage-looking gaps (2017-2018) are not a template for today: they reflected capital controls and withdrawal frictions that a backtest cannot trade through.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for how often (minutes per year) BTC prices on top-5 venues diverge by more than X bp in recent years.
- Crépellière et al. 2023 was not located directly; figure relies on Decrypt summary.

## 5. Crypto-specific manipulation patterns that make historical patterns look tradeable

### Takeaway
Crypto has well-documented manipulation that leaves fingerprints in historical data: open, coordinated pump-and-dumps on low-caps (hundreds of events, ~65% average price distortion), insider/bot manipulation of BTC itself in 2013, stablecoin-linked price support in 2017, wash-trading-as-a-service, and spoofing (documented mainly by detection research and vendors, not by prevalence studies). Patterns created by these one-off actors appear as "anomalies" in backtests but are not repeatable and are often not fillable at the printed prices.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER, 2023] Dhawan & Putniņš, "A New Wolf in Town? Pump-and-Dump Manipulation in Cryptocurrency Markets" (Review of Finance 27(3):935-975): 355 pump cases over six months; crypto manipulators openly announce pumps; average price distortions ~65%; participation explained by overconfidence and gambling preferences — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:27:y:2023:i:3:p:935-975.)
- [WP, 2018-19] Hamrick et al., "The Economics of Cryptocurrency Pump and Dump Schemes": 3,767 pump signals on Telegram and 1,051 on Discord in six months of 2018; pumps modestly successful at short-term price rises, effect diminishing over time — [CEPR DP13404](https://voxeu.org/publications/dp13404)
- [WP, 2018-19] Xu & Livshits, "The Anatomy of a Cryptocurrency Pump-and-Dump Scheme": studies Telegram pump channels and builds a model predicting which coins get pumped — [arXiv 1811.10109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10109v1)
- [PEER, 2018] BTC itself: Willy/Markus bot trades on Mt. Gox associated with ~4% daily price rises; argued to have driven much of the late-2013 $150 -> $1,000 run — [WEIS slides](https://tylermoore.utulsa.edu/weis17pres.pdf); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2018/01/23/one-person-caused-bitcoin-to-spike-from-150-to-1000-in-2013.html)
- [PEER, 2020] Griffin & Shams: Tether-flow-timed buying after downturns associated with BTC price jumps in 2017 — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:75:y:2020:i:4:p:1913-1964)
- [REG, 2024] Paid market makers (Gotbit, ZM Quant, CLS Global, MyTrade) provided wash trading/price inflation as a service for ~60 tokens — [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/eighteen-individuals-and-entities-charged-international-operation-targeting-widespread)
- [PEER, 2023] Cong et al.: wash trading temporarily distorts prices, not just volume — [NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w30783)
- [WP, Apr 2025] Spoofing: Fabre & Challet use Level-3 crypto order-book data and Hawkes-process features to detect spoofing; models ignoring how far from the best price orders are posted fail to describe the data. This is detection methodology, not a prevalence estimate — [arXiv 2504.15908](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15908v1)
- [IND] Typical spoofing/layering signals on CEXs: high cancel-to-trade ratios, large displayed orders cancelled within seconds, layered non-bona-fide orders on one side paired with real trades on the other — [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3650/market-maker/market-maker-due-diligence-detecting-spoofing-layering-and-collusive-liquidity-provision-on-cex-and-dex-venues.html) (vendor material; some pages contain odd prose — treat as secondary)
- [PRESS/ANEC, 2020] Stop/liquidation-cascade allegations: FTX's Bankman-Fried argued BTC rebounded once BitMEX went offline and its liquidation engine stopped on Mar 13 2020; BitMEX called it a "conspiracy theory" — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitmex-denies-it-made-bitcoin-price-drop-to-37k-after-going-offline)

### Inferences
- Order-book imbalance signals built on historical L2 data may be learning from spoof orders (displayed and cancelled), which a live strategy cannot trade against; snapshots at fixed intervals hide the cancellations.
- Low-cap "momentum" or "breakout" backtests partly capture pump-and-dump events, whose returns require being inside the organizing group's timing; the printed peak is typically untradeable at size.
- Historical BTC regimes (2013 Mt. Gox bots, 2017 Tether flows) may be manipulation-driven; treating them as representative training data for trend/momentum models is questionable.
- "Stop-hunting" around obvious levels is widely asserted by traders, but in this pass it is supported only by anecdote and inferred liquidation-cascade dynamics (another researcher covers liquidation mechanics).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed prevalence estimate for spoofing in BTC order books on major exchanges was found; no CFTC crypto spoofing enforcement action was located in this pass.
- No peer-reviewed study was found that directly documents deliberate stop-hunting at known liquidation levels on BTC; evidence is anecdotal/press.
- Hamrick et al. and Xu & Livshits figures come from search abstracts; full texts not reviewed.
